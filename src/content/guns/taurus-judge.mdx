The Taurus Judge is a five-shot double-action revolver, introduced in 2006 as a derivative of the
existing Taurus Tracker frame, built to fire .45 Colt cartridges and .410 bore shotshells from the same
cylinder without a barrel or cylinder swap. Taurus gave it its name after learning that judges in
high-crime parts of Miami were buying the revolver for use in their own courtrooms; the company then
renamed the model from 4410 to 4510, a number built from the two chamberings it fires (.45 Colt, .410).

Firing two very different kinds of ammunition from one barrel meant a compromise in the rifling: Taurus
cut it shallower than is typical for a handgun, trading some of the spin a single .45 Colt projectile
would otherwise get for less rapid dispersal of shot fired from a .410 shell.

Taurus later introduced the Public Defender series on the smaller Model 85 frame as a concealed-carry
variant with the same five-round, dual-chambering cylinder, and the Raging Judge, a larger revolver
chambered additionally for .454 Casull that is a separate product rather than a configuration of this one.
A short-lived Raging Judge XXVIII prototype chambered for 28-gauge shotshells was shown in 2011 but does
not appear to have reached production.

No maker specification sheet independent of Wikipedia's uncited infobox was found, so the mass, dimensions
and barrel length recorded here are estimated rather than verified; a lighter alloy-framed "Ultra-Lite"
version and longer 4-inch and 6.5-inch barrels exist but are not separately recorded. Every Judge will
also fire the shorter .45 Schofield from its .45 Colt chamber; that is a property of the two cartridges
rather than a chambering Taurus built the revolver for, so it is not listed as one.
