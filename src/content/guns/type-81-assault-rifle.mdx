China developed the Type 81 after the 1979 Sino-Vietnamese War exposed shortcomings in the People's
Liberation Army's existing small arms, the Kalashnikov-derived Type 56 assault rifle and Type 56 carbine.
Wang Zijun's design took features from both into a single new rifle rather than replacing them with a
clean-sheet weapon, keeping the short-stroke gas piston and rotating bolt this family already used while
reworking the receiver and controls.

The Type 81 was conceived as a "family weapons system" from the outset: the standard rifle, a folding-stock
Type 81-1, and a light machine gun variant with a longer barrel and bipod share the same basic action and
magazine-well dimensions, so a single production line and a common ammunition and magazine supply could
equip riflemen and squad automatic weapon gunners alike. Because the LMG variant is classed under a
different type in this site's taxonomy — a light machine gun rather than a rifle — it is not modelled here
as a variant; it would need its own entry.

The rifle armed China's regular forces through the 1980s and 1990s and was licensed to Bangladesh Ordnance
Factories, remaining a common sight across conflicts far from China itself. The People's Liberation Army
has since replaced most of its Type 81s with the bullpup Type 95 and Type 03 rifles, though the Type 81
remains in Chinese reserve and armed-police use and in first-line service with several other armed forces.
