Louis Schmeisser designed this pistol and Theodor Bergmann's company sold it, one of the earliest
commercially produced self-loading pistols and an early example of a design built specifically around
cartridges that had no other use. The earliest models needed no extractor at all: their proprietary
rimless cartridges lacked an extractor groove, so spent cases were ejected by residual gas pressure alone
rather than being pulled from the chamber mechanically, and only later production added an extractor.

Bergmann sold the pistol in several bore sizes rather than settling on one — the No. 2 in 5mm Bergmann,
the No. 3 in 6.5mm recorded as this entry's primary chambering, the No. 4 in 8×22mm Bergmann, and a later
M1897 model in 7.8mm Bergmann — each cartridge proprietary to Bergmann and useless in any other maker's
pistol. Loading followed the Mannlicher system rather than a detachable magazine: a fan-shaped cover on
the right side of the frame rotated forward to expose the fixed five-round magazine, into which a loaded
stripper clip was pressed directly.

Production numbers stayed modest by the standards of arms that followed it — roughly 2,000 No. 2 pistols
and 4,400 No. 3s were built — reflecting a design that mattered more for what it tried first than for how
widely it sold.
