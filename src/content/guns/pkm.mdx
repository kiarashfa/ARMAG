The PK is what happens when a rifle action is asked to do a machine gun's work. The bolt and carrier are
the Kalashnikov mechanism turned upside down, with the gas piston underneath the barrel, firing from an
open bolt so the chamber can cool between bursts. Around that sits a stamped sheet-steel receiver, a
quick-change barrel with a folding carry handle, and a feed system that has to pull a rimmed cartridge
backwards out of a non-disintegrating belt before pushing it forward into the chamber, because a rim
will not let it go straight through. The 1969 PKM is the lightened production version: no barrel
fluting, a stiffened receiver cover, a shorter flash hider and about 1.5 kg saved.

The PKM is carried and fired by one soldier from its own bipod while a second carries the ammunition. The
PKT, the same gun with the stock removed and a solenoid trigger fitted for mounting in a tank, is a
vehicle version of the same gun.
