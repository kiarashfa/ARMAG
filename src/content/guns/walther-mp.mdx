The Walther MP is a blowback-operated submachine gun Carl Walther GmbH produced in West Germany
from 1963 to 1983, offered in two sizes sharing the same action, cartridge and magazine: the
longer MPL (Maschinenpistole Lang) and the shorter MPK (Maschinenpistole Kurz). Its bolt design is
unusual, similar in concept to but distinct from a telescoping bolt: a hollow tubular weight rides
above the barrel, connected to a steel block underneath that performs the bolt's normal functions,
an arrangement that let Walther fit a relatively long barrel into a compact overall weapon.

The West German Navy adopted the MPL, and some domestic police forces, including the West Berlin
Police, adopted the MPL, the MPK or both, but Heckler & Koch's MP5 eventually eclipsed the Walther
MP's domestic sales and led to its discontinuation. It found a wider export market, with countries
including Brazil, Colombia and Venezuela buying the compact MPK specifically, and it was carried by
United States Navy SEALs in Vietnam and by Delta Force during Operation Eagle Claw. It has also been
documented in service across a range of other conflicts and internal security operations from the
1960s through the 1980s, including the Portuguese Colonial War, the Rhodesian Bush War and the
Lebanese Civil War.

Mass, length and barrel length figures for both variants, along with cyclic rate and magazine
capacity, come from a specialist firearms reference site and are recorded as verified. Effective
range for each variant is recorded as estimated, since Wikipedia's infobox carries no citation for
those figures. No published twist rate was found for either variant, and no photograph of the
shorter MPK appears on this site.
