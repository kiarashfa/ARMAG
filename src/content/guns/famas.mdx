The only service rifle in general issue to use lever-delayed blowback. There is no gas system: a
two-armed lever in the bolt bears against a shoulder in the receiver, and the light bolt head can only move
back by driving the heavier bolt body back several times faster through that lever. The geometry does the work
that a gas piston does elsewhere, which means fewer parts and no gas tube — and a rifle that is fussy about
chamber condition and needs fluting to help the case release.

That mechanism, plus a bullpup layout, gives a 488 mm barrel inside a rifle 757 mm long. It also gives a
cyclic rate around a thousand rounds a minute, roughly a third faster than an M16, which is why the F1 has a
three-round burst setting and why French conscripts learned to use it.

France adopted it in 1978 and replaced it with the HK416F from 2017, mainly because production had ended in
2000 and the remaining rifles could not easily take modern optics or the standard NATO magazine.

The image on this page shows a US Marine firing a FAMAS on a familiarisation range rather than a specimen
photograph; no freely licensed studio image of the rifle exists on Wikimedia Commons.
