The Błyskawica ("Lightning") is a simple-blowback submachine gun the engineers Wacław Zawrotny and
Seweryn Wielanier designed in 1943 in German-occupied Poland for the Home Army resistance, chambered
in 9×19mm Parabellum so that captured German ammunition could be used. Its parts were joined with
screws and threads rather than welded, so that it could be made in small workshops by inexperienced
engineers; neither designer had built a gun before. Its outward layout, a retractable stock with the
magazine below the gun, came from the German MP 40, and its mechanism from the British Sten.

Final assembly of many examples took place in a Warsaw workshop that officially manufactured metal
fencing, with the guns test-fired in a cellar to avoid detection. Plans were distributed to a
number of clandestine workshops across occupied Poland from November 1943, and roughly 740 were
produced between 1943 and 1944. It saw combat with Home Army fighters, most notably during the
1944 Warsaw Uprising.
