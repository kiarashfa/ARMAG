The Raging Bull is a large-frame, double-action/single-action revolver Taurus began building in 1997
around a dual-locking cylinder mechanism designed to handle heavy magnum cartridges on a single heavy
frame. A manually operated front cylinder latch on the crane, similar in principle to a Dan Wesson
revolver, gives the lockup strength comparable to the rear-latch systems used on rivals such as the Ruger
Super Redhawk and the Smith & Wesson Model 500, at lower manufacturing cost, though it requires both hands
to open the cylinder. Ported barrels and a cushioned rubber grip with a distinctive red backstrap insert
are standard across most of the line, intended to make the heavier chamberings more manageable to shoot.

The name plays on both the Taurus company's namesake constellation and boxer Jake LaMotta's "Raging Bull"
nickname, marketing the line explicitly toward hunters. Taurus discontinued the Raging Bull name in 2019 in
favour of the Raging Hunter series, a lighter revolver with the same dual-locking cylinder concept but
adding a built-in Picatinny rail for mounting a scope; the Raging Hunter won American Hunter's handgun of
the year award for 2019.

This entry covers the platform generically across both the Raging Bull and Raging Hunter lines. Its
principal chambering has been .44 Magnum, and Taurus has also offered it in .357 Magnum and .38 Special +P
(Raging Hunter only), .45 Colt, and three lighter varmint-rifle-cartridge chamberings marketed as the
Raging Hornet, Raging Bee and Raging Thirty: .22 Hornet, .218 Bee and .30 Carbine respectively, all since
discontinued, and a six-shot .41 Magnum, also discontinued. The heaviest chamberings are five-shot: .454
Casull and .500 S&W Magnum, the discontinued .480 Ruger, and .460 S&W Magnum, added to the Raging Hunter
only in 2021. The Raging Judge, a related but distinct product combining .45 Colt, .454 Casull and .410
bore shotshell capability in one cylinder, is a separate product and not part of this entry.
