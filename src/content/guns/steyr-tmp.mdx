The Steyr TMP (Taktische Maschinenpistole, tactical machine pistol) is a 9×19mm Parabellum machine pistol made by Steyr Mannlicher of Austria, designed by Friedrich Aigner in 1989, with production from 1992 until 2001. It is the original of the design that Brügger & Thomet later sold as the MP9: in 2001 Steyr sold the design to Brügger & Thomet, which developed it into that gun, and Steyr's production ended the same year.

The infobox gives the action as short recoil with a locking rotating barrel, and it is marked there as needing a citation. The magazine is a detachable box of 15 or 30 rounds. A suppressor can be fitted, and although the TMP was originally meant to be used without a shoulder stock, a fixed stock was offered later. The article's variant is the SPP (Special Purpose Pistol), a semi-automatic version with slightly longer barrel and jacket, without the forward grip, and built mainly of Nylon 66.

The users section lists the Austrian EKO Cobra, Saudi Arabia, Italy's Gruppo di Intervento Speciale, and Myanmar, where the gun was manufactured locally as the MA-13 MK-II. The see-also list links the Beretta 93R, the Heckler & Koch VP70, the KGP-9, the Milkor BXP, the Patria and the PP-2000.
