The AK-105 is a short-barreled carbine version of the AK-74M, designed by Mikhail Kalashnikov's
bureau in 1994 to replace the shorter AKS-74U, although both remain in service. It belongs to the
AK-100 family of export- and domestically-oriented Kalashnikov rifles that Izhmash introduced in the
1990s, alongside the full-length AK-101 and AK-103 chambered in other cartridges.

Compared to the full-length AK-74M and AK-101/AK-103 rifles of the same family, the AK-105 keeps
the same gas-operated, rotating-bolt action and 5.45×39mm chambering but cuts the barrel down to
roughly half the length, trading effective range for a shorter, easier-to-handle weapon. It
entered Russian military service in 2001 and has since been adopted domestically by the Russian
Army in a role other AK-100-series rifles, sold mainly for export, generally do not fill, as well
as by a number of other armed forces and law-enforcement services.
