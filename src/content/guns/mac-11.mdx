The MAC-11 is a straight-blowback submachine gun Gordon Ingram designed for the Military
Armament Corporation, entering production in 1972 as a scaled-down companion to his earlier
MAC-10. Where the MAC-10 was chambered in .45 ACP and 9×19mm, the MAC-11 shrank the design to the
smaller, lower-pressure .380 ACP, producing the smallest and lightest gun in the family at the
cost of controllability at its very high cyclic rate.

Its box-section stamped-steel receiver, cocking knob on top of the receiver rather than a
separate handle, and telescoping bolt that wraps around the rear of the barrel are all inherited
directly from the MAC-10 — the same construction shrunk to a smaller cartridge rather than a new
design. Military Armament Corporation collapsed in 1976, and production passed through a long
chain of successor companies (RPB, Cobray, SWD, and eventually MasterPiece Arms, the current
rights holder) that built the gun, and civilian semi-automatic variants of it, for decades
afterward — including the M11/9 rechambered for the more available 9×19mm Parabellum shown in
this entry's photograph.

This entry's weight, length, barrel length and cyclic rate figures all come from the cited
Wikipedia infobox, which carries no inline citation for any of them; they are recorded as
estimated rather than verified for that reason, and the cyclic rate specifically traces to a
print reference (Robert E. Walker's *Cartridges and Firearm Identification*) with no clickable
source. No manufacturer product sheet with primary figures for the original MAC-11 was found —
current MasterPiece Arms literature describes later semi-automatic pistols built on the same
platform rather than the original open-bolt submachine gun this entry describes.
