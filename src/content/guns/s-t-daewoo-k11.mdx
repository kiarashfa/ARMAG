The K11 is a South Korean combination infantry weapon developed from 2000 to 2008 by the Agency for
Defense Development with S&T Motiv, EO System, Poongsan and Hansung ILS, conceived along the same lines
as the earlier American XM29 OICW: a single bullpup unit pairing a conventional rifle with a 20×30mm
grenade launcher and a shared electronic sight. This entry covers only the rifle section, chambered for
5.56×45mm NATO; the grenade-launcher section, which fires programmable air-bursting rounds set to detonate
at a range dialled in through a laser rangefinder and ballistics computer, falls outside what this site
catalogues and is not represented in the data here.

South Korea adopted the K11 in 2008 and issued it to the Republic of Korea Army from 2010, making it the
first military to field an airburst infantry weapon as standard issue, with squads receiving two rifles
each rather than a full replacement for existing K2 rifles and their underslung K201 grenade launchers.
The United Arab Emirates bought forty for evaluation the same year. The programme was troubled from early
in its service life: a 2011 review found defects including barrel movement under fire and problems
switching between single and automatic fire, and a further fire-control fault suspended use of the roughly
900 rifles then in the field in 2014. South Korea officially cancelled the K11 programme in December 2019,
citing accuracy shortfalls and the unresolved defects.

The rifle section is a gas-operated, rotating-bolt design that feeds from standard STANAG-pattern 5.56mm
box magazines in 20- or 30-round capacity, selected through the same fire-control selector that also
switches the unit into grenade-launcher mode; the rifle and launcher share one trigger and cannot be fired
in the same instant. Its fire selector layout borrows the M16/M4 pattern in principle, with an added
position for the grenade launcher.

No source states whether the gas system is a short- or long-stroke piston design, so this page leaves that
detail unspecified rather than guess. Cyclic rate for the rifle section is recorded from an uncited
Wikipedia figure and is therefore estimated rather than verified, and no rifling twist rate for the
5.56mm barrel has been found.
