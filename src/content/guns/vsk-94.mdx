The VSK-94 (Voyskovoy Snayperskiy Kompleks, "military sniper complex", GRAU index 6V8) is a 9×39mm designated marksman rifle based on the 9A-91 assault rifle. Vasily Gryazev designed it at the KBP Instrument Design Bureau and the Tula Arms Plant manufactures it. The article gives the design year as 1995 in its text and 1994 in its infobox; the infobox's service date of 1994 is recorded here.

The article says it was meant as a cheaper alternative to the VSS Vintorez, and unlike the VSS its suppressor is not integrated: the barrel is threaded for a specially designed one. It keeps the 9A-91's long-stroke gas piston and four-lug rotating bolt and feeds from the same 20-round box magazine. It has a stamped steel receiver, a skeletonised polymer stock with an integral pistol grip and a synthetic handguard. The main optic is a 4× PSO-1, which the article says is optimised for shooting with subsonic rounds, with the 9A-91's iron sights as a back-up. The infobox lists SP-5, SP-6, PAB-9, SPP and BP loads in the 9×39mm.

The users section lists the Belarusian special forces, India's MARCOS, Russia's Spetsnaz units, FSB and OMON, and the Syrian Republican Guard and special forces; the infobox names the Second Chechen War, the Russo-Georgian War and the Syrian Civil War. The article also notes that the 9×39mm cartridge weighs about twice as much as the 9×19mm Parabellum, so that its muzzle energy is about twice that of a subsonic 9×19mm Parabellum cartridge from an HK MP5SD.

Gaps on this page: every figure is from Wikipedia's infobox, uncited there, so all are estimated. No fire modes are recorded, because the article does not state them for this rifle. No twist rate is published.
