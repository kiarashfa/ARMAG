The VSK-94 (Voyskovoy Snayperskiy Kompleks, "military sniper complex", GRAU index 6V8) is a 9×39mm designated marksman rifle based on the 9A-91 assault rifle. Vasily Gryazev designed it at the KBP Instrument Design Bureau and the Tula Arms Plant manufactures it. Wikipedia gives the design year as 1995 in its text and 1994 in its infobox; the infobox's service date of 1994 is recorded here.

It was meant as a cheaper alternative to the VSS Vintorez, and unlike the VSS its suppressor is not integrated: the barrel is threaded for a specially designed one. It keeps the 9A-91's long-stroke gas piston and four-lug rotating bolt and feeds from the same 20-round box magazine. It has a stamped steel receiver, a skeletonised polymer stock with an integral pistol grip and a synthetic handguard. The main optic is a 4× PSO-1, optimised for shooting with subsonic rounds, with the 9A-91's iron sights as a back-up. It fires the SP-5, SP-6, PAB-9, SPP and BP loads of the 9×39mm.

Its users include the Belarusian special forces, India's MARCOS, Russia's Spetsnaz units, FSB and OMON, and the Syrian Republican Guard and special forces, and it has seen service in the Second Chechen War, the Russo-Georgian War and the Syrian Civil War. The 9×39mm cartridge weighs about twice as much as the 9×19mm Parabellum, and its muzzle energy is about twice that of a subsonic 9×19mm Parabellum cartridge from an HK MP5SD.

Gaps on this page: every figure is from Wikipedia's infobox, uncited there, so all are estimated. Its fire modes are from Modern Firearms, which describes a selector giving semi-automatic and full automatic fire, kept from the 9A-91. No twist rate is published.
