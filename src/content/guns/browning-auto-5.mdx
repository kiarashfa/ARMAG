The first successful self-loading shotgun, and the only long-recoil design most people will ever
handle. Barrel and breech stay locked together and travel the entire length of the stroke — over 70 mm — before
the breech is held, the barrel returns forward alone, and the shell is extracted on the way. Watching one fire
is watching the barrel disappear into the receiver and come back.

That stroke is why the receiver is squared off at the rear and why the gun is called a humpback, and it is
also why it needs a friction ring stack under the barrel that the owner has to set for light or heavy loads.
Get it wrong and the gun either fails to cycle or beats itself apart.

Browning offered it to Winchester and to Remington on a royalty rather than a lump sum, both refused, and he
took it to Fabrique Nationale in Belgium — which is how an American design became the foundation of a Belgian
company and stayed in production for ninety-six years.

The article publishes no width, height or sight radius, and its production dates cover every licensee rather
than FN alone.
