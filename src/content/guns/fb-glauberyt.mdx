The FB Glauberyt, officially the Pistolet maszynowy wz. 1984 and commonly known by its production
designation PM-84, is a simple-blowback submachine gun a Radom design team led by Ryszard
Chełmicki won a competing-teams design process for in the early 1980s. It was developed for
armoured-vehicle crews, reconnaissance troops and police and special services, chambered in
9×18mm Makarov to match the standard sidearm cartridge of the Warsaw Pact, and built from sheet
steel subjected to heat treatment to keep production simple and inexpensive.

Fabryka Broni "Łucznik" – Radom put it into production in 1984, and it has remained in Polish
police, army and military-gendarmerie service since. A parallel 9×19mm Parabellum chambering, the
PM-84P, was developed for export and NATO-compatible customers, sharing the same action but a
different magazine and slightly larger dimensions; it is modelled on this page as a variant.
Later derivatives, the PM-98, PM-98S and PM-06, extended the same basic design further with a
longer barrel, additional rail and stock options and, in the PM-98S, a higher cyclic rate; those
differ enough in size and equipment that they are not modelled here. The type saw later combat use
well beyond Poland, including with Iraqi forces during and after the 2003 invasion.

Mass, length, barrel length, width and cyclic rate for both the PM-84 and PM-84P on this page come
from Wikipedia's infobox, which carries no inline citation for any of them, so all are recorded as
estimated rather than verified. No manufacturer specification sheet was reachable for this entry,
and no published twist rate was found either.
