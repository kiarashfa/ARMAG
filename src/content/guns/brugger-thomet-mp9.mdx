The MP9 is not a B&T design in origin. It is a development of the Steyr TMP, the Austrian machine pistol
B&T bought outright from Steyr in 2001 and put into service under its own name in 2004 — a submachine gun
small enough to holster like a large pistol rather than sling like a rifle. Where most guns this size use
a simple blowback action, relying only on the bolt's mass and a spring to hold the breech shut, the MP9
locks its barrel to the bolt through a short-recoil,
rotating-barrel mechanism: barrel and bolt travel back together for a short distance after firing, and the
barrel's rotation only releases the bolt to continue rearward once pressure has already dropped.

The design reached a wide customer base for a specialist Swiss manufacturer, entering service with units
including India's Para Special Forces, Indonesia's Kopassus, Russia's FSB Alpha Group, and police
tactical units across dozens of countries including France's RAID and Belgium's federal police, with
recorded use in the War in Afghanistan. Beyond the standard 9×19mm Parabellum chambering, B&T also builds
an MP45 variant in .45 ACP and has offered a version chambered for the low-recoil 6.5×25mm CBJ round it
developed itself; because no free dimensional standard exists yet for that cartridge on this site, the
6.5mm CBJ chambering is not recorded in this entry's data.

This entry's barrel length, and its extended and folded overall lengths, are drawn directly from B&T's
current product page and carry `verified` status; every other figure traces to the Wikipedia infobox,
mostly without an inline citation, and is recorded as `estimated`. The infobox's own weight citation points
to a manufacturer PDF that no longer resolves, and the current B&T page instead gives a heavier figure that
includes a mounted sight and loaded magazine — a different measurement from an empty gun's weight — so
neither figure is recorded as `verified` here. No twist rate is published.
