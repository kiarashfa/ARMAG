"CAR-15" named an entire family of shortened M16 derivatives Colt offered in the mid-1960s, but only one
member of that family generated real interest from the military: the Commando carbine, fielded by the US
Army and Air Force as the XM177. It answered a need the standard M16 could not — a rifle short enough to
use inside an armoured
vehicle, a boat or a helicopter without a soldier fighting the length of the barrel and stock in a
confined space.

Cutting the barrel down to roughly a third of the M16's length created a new problem the short receiver
alone could not solve, so Colt fitted a distinctive cylindrical moderator ahead of the front sight. Its
job was not to silence the weapon: the cited article describes it primarily as a counterweight against a
balance the shortened barrel had upset, which incidentally also reduced the sharp muzzle flash and made
the report closer to a full-length M16A1's than to an unsuppressed short carbine's.

Deployment stayed narrow. The Army bought 2,815 XM177E1s in 1966, issued mainly to Navy SEALs and Army
Special Forces, and later added 510 of the slightly longer XM177E2 for MACV-SOG operations in Vietnam; the
Air Force adopted its own near-identical GAU-5/A and GAU-5A/A for aircrew and security forces. The four
variants recorded here differ only in barrel length, overall length and the presence of the longer
moderator, so they share this entry's action, operating system and cartridge rather than standing as
separate entries.
