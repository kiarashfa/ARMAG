The FX-05 is Mexico's domestically designed and built service rifle, developed by the army's own
Dirección General de Industria Militar after a planned licensed-production line for the Heckler & Koch
G36 was cancelled in favour of a cheaper, wholly Mexican alternative. Development ran for sixteen months
from 2005 under the army's applied research centre, CIADTIM, and the rifle was presented publicly at the
September 2006 military parade in the hands of Mexico's Special Forces Airmobile Group, entering wider
service in 2008.

The FX-05's receiver was styled closely enough on the G36's that Heckler & Koch raised a patent
infringement claim in 2007; after inspecting the rifle's internals, the German firm concluded there was no
infringement and dropped the matter, because the mechanism inside differs substantially from the G36's.
Where the G36 uses a short-stroke gas system, the FX-05 uses a long-stroke gas piston mounted above the
barrel driving a multi-lugged rotating bolt, an arrangement closer in principle to the AK pattern, and the
external resemblance to the G36 is believed to have been deliberate, to ease commonality with the G36s
already issued to many Mexican police forces. The rifle's barrel uses polygonal rifling rather than
conventional lands and grooves, one of relatively few production service rifles built that way, and its
receiver is a carbon-fibre-reinforced polymer over a titanium frame.

It is chambered for 5.56×45mm NATO and offered in two length configurations covered here as variants: a
full-length assault rifle and a shorter carbine, which differ only in barrel length, overall length and
mass. The manufacturer has also built a short carbine with a shortened fore-end and a sharpshooter
configuration with a fixed adjustable stock in place of the standard folding one; neither is documented
with sourced figures independent of the standard configurations and neither is represented in this
entry's data. A submachine-gun-length derivative shown publicly in 2016 changes the type of arm entirely
and is likewise not covered here. The FX-05 has armed the Mexican Armed Forces and the National Guard, and
has also surfaced in the hands of organised-crime groups in Mexico's drug conflict.

No maker specification sheet for the FX-05 is published; every dimension on this page comes from an
uncited Wikipedia infobox figure and is recorded as estimated rather than verified. No source pairs a
muzzle velocity with the barrel length it was measured over, so no velocity is recorded. No rifling twist
rate is published, and no logo or wordmark for the manufacturer, a directorate of Mexico's defence
secretariat rather than a commercial brand, could be sourced.
