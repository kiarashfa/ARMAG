Heckler & Koch developed the HK33 in the 1960s primarily to sell abroad, applying the roller-delayed
blowback mechanism of its G3 battle rifle to a lighter rifle built around the smaller 5.56mm cartridge
rather than 7.62×51mm NATO. Downscaling the mechanism was not a simple resizing: the geometry that
governs how the rollers transmit force between the bolt head and the bolt carrier had to be reworked for
the lower-pressure, lighter case, since the ratios that let the G3's action delay unlocking correctly do
not scale linearly with cartridge size. The result kept the G3 family's stamped-steel receiver and lack
of a gas system, an arrangement that depends entirely on case friction, bolt mass and spring pressure to
hold the action closed long enough for pressure to drop to a safe level.

Despite reaching a wide range of export customers and remaining in production for decades, the HK33 never
matched the commercial reach of the 7.62mm G3 it was derived from. Heckler & Koch built it in several
configurations from the same basic mechanism: the fixed-stock HK33A2, the retractable-stock HK33A3, the
carbine-length HK33KA3, and the still shorter HK53, which trades reach for a compact package. All four
share this entry's action and operating system and differ only in stock and barrel length, so they are
recorded here as variants rather than separate entries.
