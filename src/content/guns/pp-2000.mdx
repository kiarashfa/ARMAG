The PP-2000 is a bullpup, straight-blowback submachine gun designed at KBP Instrument Design
Bureau in Tula between 1993 and 1997 and put into production in 2006, aimed at Russian police and
special-purpose units who needed something smaller than a rifle but harder-hitting than a
standard pistol. Its magazine sits inside the pistol grip and its bolt runs back over the top of
the barrel rather than behind it, which is what lets a gun with a full-length barrel fold down to
little more than the length of its grip and trigger group.

Its most distinctive feature is a triangular polymer frame around the trigger guard that doubles
as a foregrip, replacing the separate forend most submachine guns need, and a wire skeleton stock
that folds forward along the top of the receiver rather than to the side. KBP designed the gun
around a family of dedicated 9×19mm loads as much as around the mechanism itself: alongside
standard 9×19mm Parabellum, it is issued with 7N21 and 7N31 +P+ armour-piercing loads developed
for the same programme, neither of which is modelled as a separate cartridge entry on this site.

This entry's weight, overall length and magazine capacity come from the manufacturer's own
specification page, reached through an archived 2012 snapshot since the live site is no longer
reachable. Its cyclic rate is published there only as a 600–800 rounds-per-minute range and is
recorded here as the midpoint, per this site's rule that a range is not a single verified figure.
No source — manufacturer or Wikipedia — states a barrel length with a citation behind it, so that
figure is recorded as estimated.
