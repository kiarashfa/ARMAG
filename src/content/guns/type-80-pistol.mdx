The Type 80 is a Chinese close-combat machine pistol made by Norinco, used by the People's Liberation Army in the 1980s. It was designed in 1970 as a personal defence weapon for officers and special forces teams, to replace the Type 54, and the design was finalised in 1980. It chambers 7.62×25mm Tokarev and is influenced by the German M712 Schnellfeuer.

It is a refinement of the various Chinese copies of the imported selective-fire M712 version of the Mauser C96 that were made and used in China in the 1930s. The internal design derives from the Mauser action, with a slight recoil of the barrel enabling the locking piece under a lightweight bolt, and a selector mechanism of the later Westinger rather than the Nickl type. It resembles the C96 in having the magazine well ahead of the trigger, a slender barrel and an exposed hammer. The fixed sight is set for 50 m, said to be the maximum effective distance in bursts. A selector on the left side gives single shots or full automatic, and magazines hold 10 or 20 rounds.

The detachable metal shoulder stock can be pulled out to reveal a hidden combat knife, or a wooden holster-stock can be used instead. It had one notable disadvantage: the pistol proved unsuitable for sustained fully automatic fire, and its barrel would overheat after cycling about thirty cartridges.
