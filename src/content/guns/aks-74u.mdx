The AKS-74U was Mikhail Kalashnikov's answer to a gap between a submachine gun and a full-length rifle:
something for special forces, paratroopers, rear-echelon troops and vehicle and helicopter crews who
needed a rifle-calibre weapon but had no use for a rifle-length one. Cutting the AK-74's barrel down to
little more than a fifth of a metre solved the size problem and created a new one — a shortened gas system
reduces the propellant's dwell time in the barrel, which cost the round about 100 m of effective range and
left unburnt propellant to ignite in a large, visible fireball right at the muzzle.

Tula's engineers addressed that with a purpose-built gas block and muzzle booster rather than a simple
flash hider: an expansion chamber built into the booster raises gas pressure back up near the port to keep
the action cycling reliably on a barrel this short, while the design also works to burn off more of the
propellant that would otherwise flare visibly at the muzzle. The compromise the AKS-74U represents — some
range and some muzzle signature traded for a package that fits inside a vehicle hatch or under a jacket —
is why the design earned a role and a service life distinct from the rifle it was cut down from.
