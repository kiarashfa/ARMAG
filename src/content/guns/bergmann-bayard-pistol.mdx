The Bergmann–Bayard pistol is a recoil-operated, locked-breech semi-automatic pistol Theodor
Bergmann designed in Germany between 1901 and 1910, chambered for the 9mm cartridge he and Hugo
Schmeisser had developed alongside it — the round now known as 9×23mm Largo. Unable to find a German
manufacturer able to complete a Spanish order for 3,000 pistols, Bergmann turned to the Belgian firm
Anciens Etablissements Pieper in Herstal, which built the pistol under its own Bayard trade name and
became its lasting manufacturer.

Spain adopted the Model 1903 in 1905 as the Pistola Bergmann de 9 mm. modelo 1903, and Denmark
adopted the derived Model 1910, with an initial delivery of 4,840 pistols to the Danish Army. The
Danish M1910/21 variant remained that country's standard-issue sidearm until 1946, when the Browning
Hi-Power replaced it; Belgian production had stopped in 1914, and from 1922 to 1935 the pistol was
built in Copenhagen. Along the way it saw combat in the First World War, the Rif War, the Spanish
Civil War and the German invasion of Denmark.
