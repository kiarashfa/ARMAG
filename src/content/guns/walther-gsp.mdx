The Walther GSP (Gebrauchs Sportpistole) is a semi-automatic precision target pistol with an unlocked breech, made by Walther Sportwaffen in Germany. It shares its technical base with the Walther OSP and looks almost identical, though it is slightly longer and heavier. It was introduced in 1968 in .22 Long Rifle, and from the end of 1971 Walther also offered .32 S&W; conversion units allow either calibre to be fitted to a pistol of the other. The grips are anatomical, adjustable walnut, available for left-handed shooters, and in 1988 Walther introduced a fully adjustable two-stage trigger that owners could retrofit.

The article lists several variants. Very early pistols lacked a slide stop, which came in the 1970s, and the pistols made from 1968 to 1977 had a manual safety on the left of the frame, dropped in 1977 as unnecessary on a competition pistol. The GSP-C is the .32 S&W Long wadcutter version, built for competitions that allowed only centrefire cartridges. The GSP MV of 1976 has a matte nickel finish, and there were commemorative editions, the 25 Jahre of 1992 and the Atlanta model of 1996. The GSP Expert adds a vibration-absorbing aluminium sleeve with inertial dampers around the barrel.

The article calls it a popular choice for the 25 m events under ISSF rules. The .22 Short version was used in rapid fire pistol, the .22 Long Rifle version in the 25 m pistol and 25 m standard pistol, and the .32 version in the 25 m centre-fire pistol event.
