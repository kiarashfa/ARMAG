The MGP-15 is a Peruvian submachine gun for special forces, made by SIMA-CEFAR and developed from the earlier MGP models, with the design dated to 1990 in the infobox. The article says it was renamed from the previous MGP models because of modifications, including a longer barrel with a threaded muzzle, normally fitted with a screwed-on cap, that lets a suppressor be quickly screwed on. It chambers 9×19mm Parabellum, and as with all MGP submachine guns it takes Uzi magazines.

A folding butt stock is arranged so that, with the butt folded along the right side of the receiver, the butt plate can act as a form of foregrip. The infobox lists the action as blowback and the user as Peru. The article gives two variants: the MGP-84, an upgraded version of the MGP-15 used in close protection, and the MGP-14, a semi-automatic version of the MGP-84 with a folding forward grip, sold also as the MGP-14 Micro or MGP-14 Pistol. Jane's Guns Recognition Guide is its listed source.
