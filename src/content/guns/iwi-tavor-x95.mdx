The X95 is a revision of IWI's earlier Tavor TAR-21, keeping the bullpup layout — the action and magazine
sit behind the trigger and pistol grip rather than ahead of it — that lets a rifle carry a full-length
barrel in a body shorter than a conventional rifle's. The revision moved the charging handle from near the
muzzle on the TAR-21 back to a position close to the pistol grip, and reworked the buttstock and magazine
release, changes aimed at a rifle that handled more like a conventional carbine despite the bullpup
layout underneath.

The Israel Defense Forces selected the X95 in November 2009 to become its future standard-issue infantry
weapon, ending decades of M16 and M4 use in Israeli service. That was not the end of the story: by 2021,
reporting indicated Israeli front-line infantry units had begun reverting to the M4 in some formations,
though the article's account does not give a reason for that shift.

IWI builds the X95 across three barrel lengths on the same receiver — a short 330 mm carbine
configuration, the mid-length 380 mm rifle recorded as this entry's primary figures, and a longer 419 mm
configuration built for reach — all sharing the same gas-operated action and modelled here as variants of
one another. A 5.45×39mm-chambered X95-R variant has seen use in Ukraine, and IWI also builds pistol-calibre
9×19mm submachine-gun versions of the X95 body; because a 9mm conversion changes the operating system from
gas-operated to some form of blowback, that variant is not modelled on this entry.

Most figures in this entry trace to the Wikipedia infobox or its specifications table without an inline
citation, so they are recorded as `estimated`. No twist rate is published.
