The AR-7 is a takedown .22 Long Rifle rifle Eugene Stoner designed at ArmaLite in 1959, built from the
research and tooling behind his earlier AR-5. The AR-5 was a bolt-action survival rifle in .22 Hornet that
the U.S. Air Force adopted in 1956 as the MA-1, meant to replace the older M4 and M6 aircrew survival
weapons; the Air Force never actually issued the MA-1, because enough M4s and M6s remained in inventory,
so ArmaLite repurposed the design for the civilian market as a semi-automatic rimfire rather than a
bolt-action centrefire.

It replaces the AR-5's bolt action with a straight blowback semi-automatic mechanism, but its most
distinctive feature carries over unchanged: the rifle strips into barrel, receiver and magazine, all three
of which stow inside a hollow buttstock filled with plastic foam, buoyant enough to float. The receiver is
aluminium, the bolt steel, and the original ArmaLite barrel used a rifled steel liner inside an aluminium
shell; later production by other makers has used all-steel and composite barrels instead. ArmaLite built
the rifle from 1959 until selling the design to Charter Arms in 1973, after which it passed through
several further owners — Survival Arms, AR-7 Industries, and Henry Repeating Arms, which still sells a
version today — and was also produced abroad, including under licence in Argentina and as an adapted
pilot's survival rifle for the Israeli Air Force. This entry covers the rifle as ArmaLite itself
originally built it.

The AR-7 feeds from a detachable box magazine, standard at eight rounds with larger aftermarket magazines
available, and its reliability depends more than most semi-automatic .22s on magazine condition and
ammunition choice: makers have recommended round-nose, high-velocity loadings because flat-nosed bullets
and low-velocity ammunition are prone to jamming on the feed ramp. It remains marketed today as a
take-along utility rifle for backpackers, boaters and pilots rather than for military issue, the market
its original Air Force programme never quite created.
