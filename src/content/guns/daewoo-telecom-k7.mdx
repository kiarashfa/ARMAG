The K7 is an integrally suppressed 9×19mm submachine gun based on the Daewoo K1A, simplified by replacing
the K1A's gas system with a blowback action. The Republic of Korea Army Special Warfare Command asked for
it as a cheaper alternative to importing or licence-building the suppressed MP5SD6, and the Agency for
Defense Development and Daewoo Telecom developed it between 1998 and 2000, entering production in 2001.
Its maker's name on the type designation is already a fossil: the company has since traded as Daewoo
Precision, S&T Daewoo, S&T Motiv and, from 2021, SNT Motiv, while continuing to build the same K-series
weapons.

Mechanically the K7 is a straightforward blowback design, distinguished mainly by an integral suppressor
built around its short 134 mm barrel, which lets the whole weapon collapse to little more than 600 mm with
its stock retracted. It feeds from a proprietary 30-round box magazine, though it is also reported to
accept Uzi 30-round and Beretta PM12 32-round magazines. South Korean special forces have been its
principal user; the manufacturer's own catalogue is the only source found for its mass, and no maker
document gives a rifling twist rate, sight radius or independently verified cyclic rate for the weapon.

Cyclic rate and effective range on this page are recorded as estimated midpoints of published ranges
rather than verified single figures, and overall length, folded length and barrel length come from an
uncited Wikipedia infobox figure rather than a primary source.
