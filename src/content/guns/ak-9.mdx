The AK-9 is Izhmash's answer to the AS Val and SR-3 Vikhr: a Kalashnikov-pattern carbine built
around the same 9×39mm subsonic cartridge family, offering the SP-5 and SP-6 loads' short-range,
armour-piercing terminal performance with a quick-detachable suppressor rather than an integral
one. Design work began in the early 2000s, reportedly at the Russian Interior Ministry's request for
a rifle combining Kalashnikov-pattern reliability and controls with the maneuverability of a
dedicated suppressed carbine, and it is built on the AK-100 series' action and furniture rather than
a new design from the ground up. Alexei Dragunov, one of its designers, is quoted describing it as
firing "virtually without a sound", which is what the heavy subsonic 9×39mm bullet is for: it stays
below the speed of sound, so a suppressor has no ballistic crack left to mask.

It entered limited service around 2007, gas-operated with a rotating bolt and select-fire, fed from
a 20-round detachable magazine, and folds to a compact package with its suppressor removed. Despite
that, it has seen far more limited issue than its established rivals the AS Val and SR-3M, staying
a specialist tool for a few hundred units of Russian special and interior-ministry forces rather
than a general-issue arm.

No manufacturer specification sheet for the AK-9 is published free online. Every figure in this
entry comes from Wikipedia's own infobox, which carries no inline citation for any of its
dimensions, corroborated only by a Russian firearms-enthusiast site (armoury-online.ru) rather than
by Izhmash directly — so mass, length, barrel length and magazine capacity are recorded as estimated
rather than verified. The two sources' cyclic-rate figures do not fully agree, so no single number is
recorded for it. No sourced barrel length carries a stated test velocity, so no muzzle velocity is
recorded either. Its single photograph, from Wikimedia Commons, is the only freely licensed image
found across Commons, Smithsonian Open Access and DVIDS.
