The M240 is the US military's designation for a family of machine guns built on FN Herstal's
MAG design, engineered in Belgium in the 1950s by Ernest Vervier and already in wide
international service before the United States adopted it. The US military first fielded it
in armoured vehicle and coaxial mounts in the 1970s, well before issuing it as a dismounted
infantry weapon, which is why "M240" spans several distinct roles and configurations under one
family designation rather than a single fixed pattern.

The configuration described in this entry, the M240B, is the lightened, dismounted-infantry
version fielded from the early 2000s onward, fitted with a rail-mounted stock, pistol grip and
bipod so it could be carried and fired by a two-man infantry team rather than only from a
vehicle mount or tripod. It replaced the M60 as the US Army and Marine Corps' standard
squad-level medium machine gun, valued for a reputation for reliability that the ageing M60
fleet had not matched by the 1990s. Earlier and later M240 variants — the vehicle-mounted
M240C and the further-lightened M240L — share the same basic action but differ in furniture,
mount and, in the M240L's case, in receiver material, and this entry does not attempt to give
them separate figures.

Mechanically it is gas-operated with a long-stroke piston, firing from an open bolt, and its
gas system offers multiple adjustable ports that let a gunner trade cyclic rate for reduced
fouling or vice versa — a feature not present on the M60 it replaced. Production and support
in the United States passed from FN's own American subsidiary to other contractors over the
weapon's service life, including U.S. Ordnance and Barrett Firearms Manufacturing, while FN
Herstal in Belgium continues to build the parent MAG design for other militaries worldwide.

Every figure in this entry is transcribed from Wikipedia's infobox rather than a primary FN or
US Army source, none of it individually cited on Wikipedia itself, so all figures here are
recorded as estimated rather than verified. No muzzle velocity is recorded because no source
found ties the infobox's published 853 m/s figure to a stated barrel length, and reported
cyclic rate varies considerably with the gas regulator's setting; the lowest, base setting is
recorded here rather than the higher settings the infobox also publishes.
