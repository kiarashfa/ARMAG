West German police wrote a specification in 1976 that no existing pistol met: carried loaded and
chambered, safe if dropped, and ready to fire without operating any control. Heckler & Koch's answer was to
put the safety in the grip. Squeezing a lever across the front of the frame cocks the striker; releasing it
de-cocks. There is no manual safety, no decocking lever and no hammer.

The breech is held by gas rather than by a lock. A port near the chamber bleeds propellant gas into a cylinder
under the barrel, where it pushes a piston attached to the slide *forwards* — against the slide's rearward
travel — until pressure drops. The barrel is therefore fixed, which is why the pistol is as accurate as it is,
and the cylinder gets hot enough after fifty rounds to be a documented complaint.

It is the only gas-delayed blowback pistol to have been issued in quantity anywhere, and Heckler & Koch stopped
making it in 2008 because it cost several times what a Glock did to build.

The cited article publishes no weight, no length and no barrel length for any variant, so this entry records
none of them.
