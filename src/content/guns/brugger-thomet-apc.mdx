The APC9 is Brügger & Thomet's second-generation submachine gun, introduced in 2011 as a design the
Swiss maker built to be cheaper to produce than the intermediate-cartridge carbines that were displacing
submachine guns from police and military service at the time. It carries over the pistol-calibre
magazines B&T had already sold with its earlier MP9, but is a fresh design rather than a development of
it, with a polymer lower receiver, magazine and folding stock alongside an aerospace-grade alloy upper.

Mechanically the APC9 is a straight blowback arm firing from a closed bolt, with a proprietary hydraulic
buffer built into the receiver's back plate to soften recoil rather than the roller- or lever-delayed
systems some contemporaries use to tame a blowback pistol-calibre gun. Its barrel uses locking lugs in
the H&K MP5 style, and the trigger group is functionally the same unit fitted to the AR-15 family, which
lets it accept a wide range of aftermarket AR-pattern parts. B&T states more than half of the APC's parts
carry across its different chambering variants unchanged.

The APC9 is the pistol-calibre branch of a wider APC family; B&T also sells it rebarrelled for .40 S&W,
10mm Auto and .45 ACP, chamberings that share the same blowback action and are recorded on this page as
alternative chamberings of the same design. The APC556 and APC308 variants are a different design in
substance rather than name: they fire 5.56×45mm NATO and 7.62×51mm NATO respectively from a gas-operated
rotating-bolt action rather than the APC9's blowback, which changes both the operating system and the
primary cartridge family, so each has its own entry. In 2019 the U.S. Army's Sub Compact Weapon programme
selected the APC9K variant, and the type has since been adopted by several police and special-operations
units, including in Argentina and Lithuania.

No maker specification sheet for the APC9 was found with figures independent of Wikipedia's uncited
infobox, so the mass, length, barrel length, cyclic rate and effective range on this page are recorded as
estimated rather than verified. No magazine capacity for the .40 S&W, 10mm Auto or .45 ACP chamberings is
sourced, and no rifling twist rate is published for any chambering.
