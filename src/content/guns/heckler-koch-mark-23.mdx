The Mark 23 was built to a US Special Operations Command specification rather than designed first and
sold second. A 1989 review found special-operations units fielding 120 different weapon types and
configurations, a logistics burden SOCOM set out to reduce, and because those units often used a pistol as
a primary weapon in close quarters rather than a backup to a rifle, the command wrote a demanding
requirement for what would become the Offensive Handgun Weapon System: a full package of pistol,
suppressor and laser aiming module rather than a pistol alone, chambered in .45 ACP, built to run +P
pressure ammunition without extra wear, and required to reach a mean of 2,000 rounds between failures.

Heckler & Koch's submission cleared that bar by a wide margin in SOCOM's phase II reliability trials,
averaging over 6,000 rounds between failures and reaching more than 15,000 in its best runs, while Colt's
competing entry — built around existing M1911-derived technology — did not advance past the same testing.
SOCOM awarded Heckler & Koch a production contract in 1995 for just under 2,000 of the complete systems.
The pistol's threaded barrel and slide are built to take a dedicated suppressor without modification, one
of the specification's own requirements, which is why HK's own length figures for the Mark 23 are quoted
both with and without one attached.

This entry's weight and effective-range figures trace, via the Wikipedia infobox, to archived
