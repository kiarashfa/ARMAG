The Star Z-84 is a simple-blowback, open-bolt submachine gun Eduardo Iraegui designed in 1984, using
a telescoping bolt that wraps around the rear of the barrel to keep the weapon's overall length
short relative to its actual barrel length. Star Bonifacio Echeverria, S.A. built it in the Spanish
Basque region chambered in 9×19mm Parabellum, offering it in Largo (standard) and Corto (short)
barrel lengths that share the same action and differ only in barrel and overall length. It was
originally made for combat swimmers, and can be fired straight out of the water without its working
parts or magazine being drained.

It entered service with Spanish and other users from the 1980s and was used during the Iran–Iraq
War, among other conflicts. Star Bonifacio Echeverria went bankrupt in 1997, and Iran's
Revolutionary Guard Corps is reported to have produced the Z-84 without a licence.

Mass, length, folded length, barrel length and cyclic rate for both the Largo and Corto variants
on this page come from Wikipedia's infobox, which carries no inline citation for any of them, so
all are recorded as estimated rather than verified. No manufacturer specification sheet was
reachable, since Star Bonifacio Echeverria itself ceased operating in 1997. No published twist
rate was found for this entry.
