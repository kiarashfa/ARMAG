The 9A-91 is a compact rifle chambered for the subsonic 9×39mm cartridge and designed by the KBP Instrument Design Bureau in 1991. The article describes it as a cheaper and more versatile alternative to the SR-3 Vikhr, in use with Russian police forces, with Russia's Federal Security Service named alongside them in the infobox. Its users section lists the Saturn special-purpose unit, Belarus, Kyrgyzstan, Mongolia, Syria and Laos, and the infobox gives service from 1993.

It is gas-operated, with a long-stroke piston above the barrel and a rotating bolt with four lugs. The receiver is steel stampings, the forend and pistol grip are polymer, and the steel stock folds up and over the receiver. Early guns have the charging handle welded to the right of the bolt carrier and the safety and selector on the left above the trigger guard; current production has a folding charging handle, the selector on the right and a sight rail on the left, which takes optics. The selector offers semi-automatic and fully automatic fire. The flip-up rear sight is marked for 100 and 200 metres, but the article says the short sight base and the steep trajectory of the subsonic bullet effectively limit the rifle to about 100 metres. The infobox lists 200 m, or 400 m with an optical sight, so no effective range is recorded here.

The cartridge is the one the AS Val and VSS were built around, listed here as SP-5, SP-6 and PAB-9 loads. The VSK-94 is a sniper rifle based on the 9A-91, and is described separately.
