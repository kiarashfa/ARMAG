The SG 540 was SIG's second attempt at a lightweight 5.56×45mm rifle. Its predecessor, the SG 530-1, used
a gas-assisted roller-delayed blowback action that proved too complicated and expensive to build, so in
1969 SIG replaced it with a simpler and more robust gas-piston rotating-bolt mechanism derived from the
Soviet AK-47's, developed through the early 1970s and put into production from 1977. SIG designed it
largely as a private-venture export rifle and a possible replacement for its own 7.5×55mm SG 510 in Swiss
service, though Swiss federal restrictions on arms exports meant its series production was carried out at
Manurhin's plant in Mulhouse, France.

The rifle fires from a closed bolt with a long-stroke gas piston and a two-lug rotating bolt actuated by
a cam slot in the carrier, and its gas regulator has three settings — one for rifle grenades, one for
normal use, and one for fouled or extreme conditions. Its receiver and most furniture make heavy use of
stamped and pressed steel and polymer parts rather than machined ones, a deliberate cost-saving choice for
an export design competing on price. Portugal's INDEP later licensed production of the carbine variant in
1988, and Chile's FAMAE has manufactured the SG 540, SG 542 and SG 543 domestically under licence for the
Chilean Armed Forces, work that also produced the SAF, a 9×19mm submachine gun FAMAE developed from the
SG 540's layout.

This entry covers the SG 540 and its 5.56mm carbine derivative, the SG 543, which is recorded here as a
variant differing only in barrel length, overall length and mass, with a folding stock in place of the SG
540's fixed one. The family's third member, the SG 542, is chambered for the full-size 7.62×51mm NATO
cartridge rather than 5.56mm, and has its own entry. The SG 540 series has seen use with military, police
and irregular forces in conflicts including the Lebanese Civil War, the Somali Civil War and Syria's civil
war, and with organised-crime groups in Brazil.
