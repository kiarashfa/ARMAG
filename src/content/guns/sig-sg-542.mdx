The SG 542 is the 7.62×51mm NATO member of SIG's SG 540 family, the full-power battle rifle built on
the same long-stroke gas piston and two-lug rotating bolt as the 5.56mm SG 540 and SG 543. Like the rest
of the family, it was designed in Switzerland but, because of Swiss federal restrictions on arms
exports, series-produced at Manurhin's plant in Mulhouse, France; FAMAE in Chile still builds it under
licence for the Chilean armed forces.

The encyclopedia puts the differences from the SG 540 down mainly to the larger, more powerful cartridge.
The mechanism is otherwise the family's: a closed-bolt, hammer-fired action with a three-position gas
regulator, one setting of which serves for launching rifle grenades from the flash suppressor, and a
combined safety and selector for single shots, three-round bursts and automatic fire. The SG 542 normally
takes 20- or 30-round box magazines, with 5- and 10-round magazines also made, and its rear diopter drum
adds a 600 m setting to the SG 540's four. FAMAE has also built it for the Canadian civilian market, and
it has been issued to the armies of Bolivia and Ecuador among others.

Every figure on this page is an uncited encyclopedia figure given for the SG 542 within the SG 540
family's article, and is recorded as an estimate; the cyclic rate is the family's published range, not
one measured on this model. No maker specification sheet, rifling twist or muzzle velocity paired with a
stated barrel length was found.
