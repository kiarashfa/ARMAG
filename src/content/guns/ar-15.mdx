This is a platform page, not a product page. Nobody manufactures an AR-15 platform; hundreds of
companies manufacture rifles that conform to it, and the reason the conformity holds is that the
interfaces were fixed by a military specification and then never protected. Eugene Stoner's design
passed from ArmaLite to Colt in 1959 and Colt's patents ran out; the magazine well, the barrel
extension, the buffer tube thread and the receiver pin spacing became a de facto open standard that any
machine shop could work to.

The consequence is a market shaped like no other in small arms. An upper receiver from one company sits
on a lower from another, both feed from a magazine specified by a NATO agreement, and the serialised
part in United States law is the lower receiver, which contains no barrel and fires nothing on its own.

The entries grouped here are computed from the family reference on each one. Nothing on this page owns
them: Colt's M4 carbine and Smith & Wesson's M&P15 are separate arms from separate makers, and this page
records only what they have in common.
