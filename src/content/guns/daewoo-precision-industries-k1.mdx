South Korea developed the K1 from 1976 to replace the ageing M3 submachine gun in special-forces and
close-quarters roles, a narrower brief than the K2 rifle developed alongside it to replace the M16A1
across the wider army. The two share a family resemblance and a maker, but the K1 is built around a
shorter barrel and a telescoping stock for a role where compactness mattered more than reach.

The original K1 had a real fault: its short barrel produced excessive recoil, muzzle flip and flash, and
early users judged the stock too weak for hard use. Daewoo's fix was a new flash suppressor with three
vents in its upper-right quadrant that cut flash to roughly a third of the early gun's and helped control
muzzle rise under rapid fire, along with a stronger stock. Every K1 still in service was eventually brought
up to that K1A standard, which is why this entry — and every dimensional figure Wikipedia publishes for
the type — describes the K1A configuration rather than the unmodified original.

The rifle chambered .223 Remington through most of its production run and moved to 5.56×45mm NATO from
2014, a change reflected in South Korea's broader move toward NATO ammunition standardisation.
