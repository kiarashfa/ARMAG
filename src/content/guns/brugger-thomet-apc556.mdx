The APC556 is the 5.56×45mm NATO member of B&T's Advanced Police Carbine family, first shown at the
SHOT Show in 2015. It shares the family's controls and more than half of its parts with the
pistol-calibre APC9, but not its mechanism: where the APC9 is a straight blowback submachine gun, the
APC556 is gas-operated, with a short-stroke piston driving a rotating bolt, which makes it a separate
rifle rather than another chambering of the same one.

B&T built it as a police carbine meant to be shared between officers on different shifts, and its
datasheet puts the emphasis there: a safety that can be applied with the hammer down, unlike an
AR-pattern rifle's, ambidextrous charging handle, bolt release and magazine catch, a folding stock that
also adjusts for length over body armour, and the family's hydraulic buffer to soften recoil. It feeds
from standard STANAG magazines. The maker offers it with a 30.8 cm or 26.4 cm barrel, as a selective-fire
rifle or a semi-automatic-only "Carbine", and as a shorter PDW that is made only with automatic fire;
B&T's datasheet describes the selector's settings as safe, single and burst. A semi-automatic civilian
model is sold as the APC223, and Lithuania's ORKA tactical unit is among its users.

Every dimension, mass, twist and cyclic rate on this page comes from B&T's own datasheet, read through a
permanent archived copy. The datasheet gives no muzzle velocity, and no source consulted states whether
its "weight empty" figures include the supplied magazine.
