The British Army adopted the Webley in 1887 to replace the .476 Enfield, whose American-designed
extraction system had never worked reliably in service. What made the Webley an improvement was its
top-break frame: pulling a catch and tipping the barrel down on a hinge simultaneously operates a star
extractor that lifts every spent case out of the cylinder at once, rather than requiring each case to be
picked out by hand or pushed free with a rod. For a soldier reloading under pressure, that difference — one
motion to clear the cylinder rather than six — was the point of the design.

The revolver went through several Marks over three decades of British service, each a refinement of the
same top-break mechanism, before the Mk VI was adopted in 1915 as the pattern that would carry the design
through the First and Second World Wars. Its double-action trigger let it be fired either by a long,
heavy pull that both cocks and releases the hammer, or by manually cocking the hammer first for a lighter,
more precise single-action shot. The .455-calibre line was formally retired from British service in 1947,
though the smaller-calibre Mk IV .38/200 descendant remained in official use until 1963, and enough
wartime stock remained in circulation that Webleys turned up in British hands for years after their
official retirement. Mk VI revolvers sold as surplus into the United States from the late 1950s were
commonly altered rather than fed: with .455 ammunition scarce there, importers shaved the cylinders to
take .45 ACP. That is a modification made to the gun after it left service, not a chambering Webley
built, so it is recorded here as history rather than as one of the revolver's cartridges.

This entry's weight, length, barrel length and effective range all trace to the Wikipedia infobox without
an inline citation and are recorded as `estimated`. It records the Mk VI, the best-documented and
longest-serving of the family's several Marks; earlier and later Marks of the same basic top-break design
are not modelled here as separate variants. No twist rate is published for the revolver.
