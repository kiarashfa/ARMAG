The PP-90 is a folding blowback submachine gun that the KBP Instrument Design Bureau in Tula developed for special units of the Russian interior ministry (MVD). Its defining feature is that it stows as a featureless block, about 270 × 90 × 32 mm, with the pistol grip and magazine folded up under the barrel and covered by the folded stock, so that it can be carried concealed and brought into action quickly in awkward circumstances.

It fires the 9×18mm Makarov cartridge by straight blowback and, in its standard form, in automatic fire only. Unfolding the stock swings the pistol grip, trigger and trigger guard into position, and the magazine sits in a well inside the hollowed grip, with its catch at the heel of the grip. The internal mechanisms are arranged so that the weapon cannot fire while folded or while the stock is not fully deployed, and a drop safety prevents discharge if it is dropped with a round chambered. A selector-safety on the left of the receiver has a safe position and an automatic position. The cocking handle sits at the rear of the receiver, reached from beneath the stock. Sights are thin sheet-metal flip-up notch and post elements that fold flat against the top cover, and the muzzle is threaded to accept a sound suppressor. A holster that hangs from a duty belt or shoulder rig carries the gun with spare magazines and a cleaning brush.

Related designs are the PP-92, chambered for the 9×19mm Parabellum; the PP-90M, a selective-fire version; and the PP-93, a version that does not fold. Those are noted here rather than modelled as variants. It is used by Russia and has been in service since 1990. Its layout is conceptually similar to the American Ares FMG and the later Magpul FMG-9.
