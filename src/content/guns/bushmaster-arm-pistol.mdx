The Bushmaster Arm Pistol was a 5.56×45mm NATO firearm produced by the Gwinn Firearms Company and later by Bushmaster Firearms. The article describes it as a new design, with a rotating bolt and a long-stroke gas piston similar to those of the AK-47, though it used some AR-15 parts and took STANAG magazines. The infobox calls it a bullpup pistol, a personal defence weapon and a carbine, and the article notes it could be seen as either a long pistol or a compact carbine rifle.

The infobox gives production from 1972 to 1990, while the article text says production of the pistol ended in 1988 and of the rifle in 1991, after Quality Products Company acquired Bushmaster the previous year; the two statements do not agree, so only the start year is recorded. The article names no other users or variants beyond the pistol and rifle versions, and its see-also list places it beside the Magpul PDR, the OTs-14-1A-02 and the TKB-022PM as short bullpup designs.

Gaps on this page: weight, length and barrel length come from the infobox, uncited there, so are estimated. No fire modes are recorded, because the article does not state them. No cyclic rate, twist rate or effective range is published. The photograph is a low-resolution view of a museum wall, the only one found.
