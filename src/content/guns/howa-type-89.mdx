The Type 89 replaced the Type 64, a 7.62×51mm battle rifle Japan had carried for twenty-five years by the
time an assault-rifle replacement entered service in 1989. The switch followed a pattern set decades
earlier elsewhere: as armies moved from full-power battle-rifle cartridges to intermediate rounds, Japan
brought its own service rifle in line with 5.56×45mm NATO rather than continue building around the
heavier 7.62mm case.

Howa builds it under Japan's long-standing arms-export ban, which has kept the Type 89 a rifle built
solely for Japanese service — it has never been exported or licensed abroad, unlike most rifles adopted
by a similarly sized armed force. That constraint has kept total production modest compared with Western
rifles built for both domestic and export markets, though the article gives no production total to compare
directly.

Mechanically the rifle follows the short-stroke gas piston and rotating bolt layout common to many
assault rifles of its generation, offered with either a fixed polymer stock or, as the Type 89-F variant
recorded here, a side-folding stock that shortens the rifle without changing its action, barrel or
cartridge.
