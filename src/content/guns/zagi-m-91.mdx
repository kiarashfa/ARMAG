The Zagi M-91 is a Croatian submachine gun created in 1991, when the breakup of Yugoslavia left Croatia with few weapons to arm its forces in the Croatian War of Independence. An embargo then prevented the new state from buying equipment abroad, so weapons had to be designed locally. It was made by Likaweld, a Zagreb machine tools factory also called the First Croatian Weapons Factory, which is now bankrupt. The name probably refers to Zagi, the squirrel mascot of the 1987 Summer Universiade in Zagreb.

The mechanism is based on the Sten, famous for its simplicity, and works by open-bolt blowback. The magazine is based on the MP40's dual-stack, single-feed magazine, and the extending stock resembles that of the American Grease gun. Unlike its Second World War predecessors, it has a partly plastic receiver and handle.

The guns were of uneven quality and unreliable, and production was soon discontinued. Some went as military aid to Bosnia and Herzegovina in 1992, where the Bosnian army used them and copied the gun in very small numbers at the Pobjeda factory in Goražde as the Bosanski Zagi (Bosnian Zagi), with a wooden lower receiver and handle in place of the plastic one. Croatia is its only user; the Pleter 91 is another homemade Croatian firearm of the period.
