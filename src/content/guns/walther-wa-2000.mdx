The WA 2000 is a semi-automatic bullpup sniper rifle Walther designed in response to the Munich massacre
at the 1972 Summer Olympics. Development ran through the late 1970s, production began in 1978 and the
rifle was brought to market in 1981, and its users were German police units.

Its most visible feature is its bullpup layout, which places the bolt behind the grip so the barrel can run
the full length of the weapon without extending the rifle's overall length — a way of keeping a full-length
barrel's accuracy in a shorter package. The barrel itself is clamped only at the front and rear, isolated
from the rest of the stock so nothing else touches or distorts it, and fluted to help it shed heat and
resist vibration. It is a gas-operated, self-loading design firing from a closed bolt with a seven-lug
rotating bolt head, feeding from a single-stack box magazine, and it carries no iron sights at all — Walther
designed it around a Schmidt & Bender 2.5–10× telescopic sight from the outset. Its primary chambering,
.300 Winchester Magnum, was chosen specifically for consistent accuracy across a range of distances, and
Walther also built barrels for 7.62×51mm NATO and 7.5×55mm Swiss.

Only 176 were built before production ended in 1988: reviewers and later writers have generally agreed it
was too expensive to sell in volume and not robust enough for military field use, positioning it instead
for law-enforcement sale, which limited its market from the start. That rarity has made surviving examples
valuable collector's items, with auction prices in the tens of thousands of dollars.

Its unloaded mass and magazine capacity for the 7.62×51mm NATO chambering come from Walther USA's own
archived owner's manual and are recorded as verified; overall length, barrel length, effective range and
the .300 Winchester Magnum and 7.5×55mm Swiss magazine capacities come from Wikipedia's own uncited infobox
and are recorded as estimated. No rifling twist rate is published for any of its three chamberings.
