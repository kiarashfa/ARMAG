The most-produced American small arm of the Second World War, and the one that is hardest to argue
about because people keep comparing it to the wrong thing. It was not meant to replace the M1 Garand. It was
meant to replace the pistol carried by men whose hands were full of something else, and against a pistol it
is an enormous improvement: three times the muzzle energy, four times the useful range, and a weight of
2.36 kg that a mortar crewman will actually keep with him.

Winchester's short-stroke tappet gas system is the mechanically interesting part — a piston moves about
3 mm, strikes the operating slide and stops. David Williams patented it, and it turns up afterwards in the
Ruger Mini-14, the SKS and a long line of others.

In Korea every version of the carbine acquired a reputation for jamming in extreme cold, eventually
traced to weak return springs, parts freezing in overly viscous lubricants and too little recoil
impulse from the cartridge at subzero temperatures.

The article's rate-of-fire figure belongs to the selective-fire M2 variant rather than to this rifle, so no
cyclic rate is recorded here.
