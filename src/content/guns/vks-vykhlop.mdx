The VKS (ВКС), also called the VSSK and Vykhlop ("exhaust", the name of the development programme), is a Russian bullpup, straight-pull, magazine-fed sniper rifle chambered for the subsonic 12.7×55mm STs-130 cartridge. It was developed around 2002 by CKIB SOO, the Central Design Bureau of Sporting and Hunting Weapons, for the special forces of the FSB, with service from 2004 per the infobox, and the article says it has also been used in Ukraine in the Russo-Ukrainian war.

The article says it is meant for special operations needing silent firing and penetration much better than the 9×39mm VSS provides, and names combatants in heavy body armour or behind cover as typical targets. It uses an integral suppressor. The cartridge was designed alongside the rifle, as the 12.7×55mm STs-130 entry on this site explains, and the article gives a cartridge overall length of 97 mm, consistent with that entry's figure for the VKS's own loads. It lists STs-130 variants for standard use, training, increased accuracy (a 59 gram bullet and a solid bronze bullet) and increased penetration (a 76 gram bullet), and claims an accuracy of 1 minute of angle at 100 metres with precision bullets.

The infobox gives folding iron sights or a telescopic sight, with a Picatinny rail for other optics. The article's external links mention photographs of what may be a different variant or a competing rifle from the same programme, with a removable muzzle suppressor.

Gaps on this page: every figure is from Wikipedia's infobox, uncited there, so all are estimated. No cyclic rate is applicable, and no twist rate is published.
