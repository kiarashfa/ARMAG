The VKS (ВКС), also called the VSSK and Vykhlop ("exhaust", the name of the development programme), is a Russian bullpup, straight-pull, magazine-fed sniper rifle chambered for the subsonic 12.7×55mm STs-130 cartridge. It was developed around 2002 by CKIB SOO, the Central Design Bureau of Sporting and Hunting Weapons, for the special forces of the FSB, entered service in 2004, and has also been used in Ukraine in the Russo-Ukrainian war.

It is meant for special operations needing silent firing and much better penetration of body armour and cover than the 9×39mm VSS provides. It uses an integral suppressor. The cartridge was designed alongside the rifle, and its published overall length of 97 mm is consistent with the figure for the VKS's own 12.7×55mm STs-130 loads. STs-130 variants exist for standard use, training, increased accuracy (a 59 gram bullet and a solid bronze bullet) and increased penetration (a 76 gram bullet), and an accuracy of 1 minute of angle at 100 metres is claimed with precision bullets.

It takes folding iron sights or a telescopic sight, with a Picatinny rail for other optics.
