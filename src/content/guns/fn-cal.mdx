The FN CAL, short for Carabine Automatique Légère ("Light Automatic Carbine"), is a gas-operated,
select-fire rifle FN Herstal designer Ernest Vervier designed between 1963 and 1966, chambered in
5.56×45mm. FN developed it after noting the growing commercial success of the cheaper, simpler
Heckler & Koch G3, and concluded that any future rifle of its own would need fewer expensive,
precision-machined parts to compete; the CAL's design accordingly replaced some machined parts
with less expensive castings and stampings, using a rotating bolt with an interrupted-thread
locking arrangement.

Despite that cost-reduction effort, the CAL remained relatively expensive and mechanically complex
to produce, and it met little sustained commercial success: roughly 30,000 were built between 1966
and 1975 before FN dropped it in favour of the simpler, cheaper FN FNC. It found buyers in Belgium,
Colombia, Gabon, Lebanon, Mexico, Morocco, Myanmar, Nicaragua and Thailand, among others, and was
demonstrated to and evaluated by several other militaries, including France and Iran, without
being adopted by either. A small number were also sold on the American civilian market.
