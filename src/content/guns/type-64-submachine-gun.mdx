The Type 64 submachine gun is a simple-blowback, open-bolt weapon China's State Factory 66
developed together with the separate Type 64 suppressed pistol for special-forces and
reconnaissance use. Unlike many suppressed firearms that adapt an existing production gun, the
Type 64 was designed as a suppressed weapon from the outset, built around a dedicated subsonic
cartridge, also called 7.62×25mm Type 64, to keep the action quiet without a supersonic crack from
the bullet itself.

It can also fire standard 7.62×25mm Tokarev ammunition, though doing so wears its integral
suppressor faster than the subsonic round does. The mechanism is an amalgam of existing designs: the
bolt of the PPS submachine gun and the trigger group of the ZB vz. 26, grafted onto an
AKS-47-pattern receiver. Some were exported to North Vietnam, where small numbers were issued to
armed police as the K64, and Chinese troops used it in the 1979 Sino-Vietnamese War before the Type
85 replaced it in PLA service.

Mass, length, folded length, barrel length and cyclic rate on this page come from Wikipedia's
infobox, which carries no inline citation for any of them, so all are recorded as estimated
rather than verified. Its introduction year follows the Chinese arms-naming convention that gives
the weapon its "64" designation, rather than a separately documented adoption date. No published
