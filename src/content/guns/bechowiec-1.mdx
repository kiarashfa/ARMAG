The Bechowiec-1 was a Polish Second World War submachine gun designed in 1943 by Henryk Strąpoć, a village blacksmith and self-taught gunsmith, for the Bataliony Chłopskie (Peasants' Battalions) resistance organisation. The Opatów district headquarters lacked machine guns and decided to organise serial production; the finished guns were assembled in Strąpoć's village workshop at Czerwona Góra, with parts made at a metalworks in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski. The name comes from bechowcy, the informal name for members of the organisation.

The first two guns were completed in January 1944 and about 20 were in production when German pressure brought work to a halt. Eleven were completed at Czerwona Góra by July 1944 and at least two more in Jan Swat's workshop in Broniszowice, about 13 in total. They went to Bataliony Chłopskie and Ludowa Straż Bezpieczeństwa units, mostly around Opatów. Only one Bechowiec-1 is thought to survive, in the Polish Army Museum in Warsaw. A later gun of the same name, the Bechowiec-2, was patterned after the Sten and is a separate design.

Because Strąpoć had little experience with machine guns and no direct pattern to copy, the gun has features closer to a semi-automatic pistol, and it is sometimes called a machine pistol. It has a slide, fires from a closed breech, which helped accuracy in single shots, and has an internal hammer and a safety that prevents firing with the breech partly open. The slide is drawn back by a transport belt fixed under the barrel. A three-position external control sets safe, single shots or automatic fire. It had no stock. It fired 9×19mm Parabellum, which could be obtained from German soldiers or in armed actions, and the last three or four guns used 7.62×25mm Tokarev. Markings read S.H. w.44 on the left (Strąpoć Henryk, pattern 1944) and B.H on the right.
