The K2 has armed the Republic of Korea's conscript army since 1985, and it was built to answer a very
specific supply problem. South Korea already built the M16A1 under licence from Colt, but the agreement
covered too few rifles to equip a force counted in millions of reserves, and the fees made arming them
that way expensive; an indigenous programme ran at the Agency for Defense Development from 1972 through
seven prototype generations before the design was fielded. The result
takes its fire control group and bolt carrier from the M16 and its gas system — a long-stroke piston above
the barrel, rather than the M16's direct impingement — from the Kalashnikov pattern, wrapped in a
polymer-covered, side-folding-stock body externally closer to the AR-18. It feeds from the same STANAG
magazine as the M16 it stood alongside, so the two rifles could draw from one logistics chain even where
they shared nothing else mechanically.

Production has run continuously under a company that has changed its name six times without changing
what it makes: Daewoo Precision Industries built the K2 from 1982, and the current manufacturer, SNT
Motiv, is the same firm after a string of corporate reorganisations. The rifle has seen combat with South
Korean forces in the Gulf War, the Iraq War and UN peacekeeping deployments, and it has been exported or
licensed to a number of other militaries and police forces. From 2015 the rail-equipped K2C1 upgrade —
recorded here as a variant, since it keeps the same action, operating system and 465 mm barrel — began
replacing the original K2 in front-line service. A separate, shorter-barrelled K2C carbine with a
retractable stock exists for roles where the full-length rifle is impractical; because it is classed as a
carbine rather than a rifle, it is not modelled as a variant on this entry. Civilian semi-automatic-only
export versions have also been sold under other names, including a variant chambered for 7.62×39mm rather
than 5.56mm NATO.

This entry has no verified weight, length or barrel-length figure: the only figures published for them
trace to SNT Motiv's own product catalogue, which Wikipedia cites but which this survey could not itself
read as a spec table, so they are recorded as estimated rather than confirmed. The cyclic rate is published
only as a 700–900 rounds/min range and is recorded here at the midpoint. No muzzle velocity or twist rate
for the K2 specifically is published with a stated barrel length, so neither is recorded, and no free
dimensional standard exists for 5.56×45mm NATO independent of its commercial equivalent.
