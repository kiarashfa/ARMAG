The OTs-33 Pernach is a Russian selective-fire pistol chambered for the 9×18mm Makarov, derived from the 5.45 mm OTs-23 Drotik. It was developed in 1995 by Igor Stechkin at the TsKIB SOO design bureau and went into production at the KBP Instrument Design Bureau, to replace the Stechkin APS in OMON and other units of the Russian interior ministry (MVD) and paramilitary forces. It is also called the SBZ-2, from the names of the KBP team: Stechkin, Baltser and Zinchenko.

The designers were asked to fix the difficulty of controlling the APS in automatic fire and to simplify it. The result has a squared outline that is simpler and cheaper to make, and a blowback action with an unusual feature. It has no positive lock between slide and barrel, but the barrel separates from the slide as they travel rearward, the spent case is ejected, and a spring then drives the slide back onto the barrel to chamber a fresh round. Barrel and slide then continue rearward together for about 5 mm. This improved accuracy in automatic fire, reduced felt recoil and slowed the cyclic rate without the separate rate reducer the APS needs. It also lets the pistol use both the standard 57-N-181S and the more powerful 57-N-181SM loads of the Makarov cartridge without a locked breech. Some of the propellant gas is vented through a ported cavity in the slide, which acts as a compensator.

The pistol is hammer-fired with a double-action trigger. A selector on each side of the slide has safe, semi-automatic and fully automatic positions, and lowering it to safe safely lowers the hammer. A folding metal shoulder stock screws onto the base of the grip, there is an accessory rail under the barrel, and the pistol can take a sound suppressor. Magazines hold 18 rounds, or 27 as an option. It is used by Russia, and has been produced and in service since 1996.
