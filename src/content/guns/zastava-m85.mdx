The Zastava M85 is a carbine designed and produced by Zastava Arms of what was then Yugoslavia. It is a shortened version of the Zastava M80, which in turn succeeded the Zastava M70 assault rifle, and the article says it is practically the same as the M92, the carbine version of the M70, the only differences being the calibre and the magazine design. Like the M80, it was meant to be a new weapon for the Yugoslav People's Army, but the breakup of Yugoslavia disrupted production, and the article says it is now produced largely for commercial sales and export.

The article describes it as an AK-pattern rifle that borrows from the Soviet AKS-74U carbine and is chambered for the 5.56×45mm. It is gas-operated, air-cooled, magazine-fed, with selective fire, and its rotating bolt is listed in the infobox. It is distinguished from other AK-family rifles by a polymer pistol grip, its 5.56×45mm magazine and a longer "Yugo" handguard with three cooling vents instead of the usual two, and it has a flash hider copied directly from the AKS-74U. Its receiver is stamped from 1.5 mm sheet steel, thicker than the 1.0 mm used in most AK derivatives, which the article says gives increased durability at the cost of weight.

The derivatives it lists are the M80, the M90 (an improved version of the original rifle) and the M92, chambered for the Soviet 7.62×39mm. None of them is modelled here.
