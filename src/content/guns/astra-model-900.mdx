The Astra Model 900 is a Spanish copy of the German Mauser C96, made by Astra-Unceta y Cia from 1927. It looks like the C96 and carries the same detachable shoulder stock that doubles as a holster, but its internal parts do not interlock the way Mauser's do. It uses the same 7.63×25mm Mauser cartridge and the same magazine capacity, and it works as the C96 does, by short recoil.

The Spanish copies were generally meant for export to China. Once the Sino-Japanese war began, the Astra 900s that remained were used in the Spanish Civil War, and some were sold to Germany between 1940 and 1943. Production ran until 1941, with a pause in 1937 and 1938, and a final batch was assembled from spare parts in 1951. About 21,000 Model 900s were made, for China, Latin America, Spanish Republicans and the Wehrmacht, and about 35,000 weapons of all versions. Its users were the Republic of China, the Empire of Japan, Nazi Germany and the Second Spanish Republic, where the Astra F was issued to the Guardia Civil.

The numbered models after the 900 are machine pistols. The 901 had a 10-round magazine, the 902 a lengthened barrel and a fixed 20-round magazine, and the 903 a removable one. The 904 added a mechanism to slow the rate of fire, and the 904E was the same gun in 9mm Largo. The 904 is comparable to the Mauser M712 Schnellfeuer, which also has a detachable magazine and automatic capability. These are recorded as variants because they share the 900's action and operating system. Eight Model 900s, twelve 902s and nine Astra 400s, all richly engraved, were presented to Joseph Stalin in 1937.

Gaps on this page: the published weight, length and barrel length are for the 903 only, so the base model has no dimensions here.
