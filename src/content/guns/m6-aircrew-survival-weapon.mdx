The M6 aircrew survival weapon is a break-action, superposed combination gun the US Army Air
Forces' Air Materiel Command developed in 1946 and formally designated Rifle-Shotgun, Survival,
Caliber .22/.410, entering service with the newly independent US Air Force in 1952. It pairs a
.22 Hornet rifle barrel above a .410 bore shotgun barrel in a single folding weapon, intended not
as a combat arm but as compact survival equipment a downed aircrew member could carry to take
small game or birds.

Ithaca Gun Company and Springfield Armory, Inc. manufactured it, and its skeletonized wire stock
folds the weapon down to about half its extended length for storage in an aircraft survival kit.
A compartment in the stock held nine rounds of .22 Hornet ammunition and four .410 shotgun shells
for the airman to carry alongside the gun itself. It remained in Air Force service into the 1970s.
