Eugene Stoner designed the Stoner 63 after leaving ArmaLite, built around one core idea: a common
receiver and bolt group that could be reconfigured, by swapping barrel, stock and feed modules, into a
rifle, a carbine, an automatic rifle, or a belt-fed light, medium or fixed machine gun. Where the AR-15 and
AR-10 had been single-purpose weapons, the Stoner 63 let one action serve every squad role a rifle
platoon needed, built by Cadillac Gage rather than ArmaLite itself.

Its combat record is tied closely to one user: Navy SEALs adopted the light-machine-gun configuration in
Vietnam as the Mark 23 Mod 0 and favoured it enough that the type is still associated with SEAL small-unit
tactics of the era, while Marine Corps trials in 1967 tested several configurations including the LMG and a
medium-machine-gun variant. The wider system never won a general-issue contract. Army trials found
reliability problems tied to ammunition tolerances the design was unusually sensitive to, and the whole
modular system demanded more cleaning and maintenance than the M16 it would have to outperform to justify
the switch. Once American withdrawal from Vietnam removed the urgency behind a new infantry weapon, the
programme lapsed, and the belt-fed role it had filled for the SEALs eventually passed to the M249 by the
late 1980s.

This entry models the box-magazine Rifle configuration as the primary entry, with the Carbine, Commando and
squad-support AR configurations recorded as variants since they share the same receiver, gas system and
magazine feed and differ only in barrel length, stock and weight. The belt-fed LMG, MMG and FMG
configurations are not modelled here: they use a different feed system, and the ranges published for their
rate of fire and effective range span far more than the box-fed configurations, so treating them as one
data point would misstate all of them.

Every figure in this entry comes from the Wikipedia article's specifications table or infobox without an
inline citation, so each is recorded as `estimated`. No twist rate is published.
