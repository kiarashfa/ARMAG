China received a licence and a full set of drawings for the Soviet AK in 1956, and State Factory 66 at
Beian began building it as the Type 56. The early rifles are milled-receiver copies of the Soviet Type
3; production moved to a stamped receiver in the 1960s in step with the AKM, without a change of
designation, which is why two rifles bearing the same three characters can differ in their most
structural component.

Two features are Chinese rather than Soviet and are the usual way of telling one apart at a glance: a
permanently attached folding spike bayonet under the muzzle, and a fully hooded front sight rather than
the Soviet open-sided post.

Sourcing an arm like this is the hard case, and this entry is in the pilot batch to prove it. There is
no manufacturer datasheet, no service manual in a language the site can cite, and no published
production figure from the state that built ten million of them. Everything below rests on one
encyclopaedia article read at a pinned revision, and the entry is marked accordingly.
