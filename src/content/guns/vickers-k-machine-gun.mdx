The Vickers K, officially the Vickers Gas Operated (G.O.), is a gas-long-stroke machine gun
Vickers-Armstrongs developed from 1935 as a development of the Vickers-Berthier light machine gun
the Indian Army had adopted in 1932. It was built specifically as a flexible aircraft gun: light
enough for a gunner to swing and aim by hand from an open cockpit, with an adjustable cyclic rate
as high as 1,200 rounds per minute so a gunner had the best chance of hitting an attacking fighter
in the brief window such an engagement allowed.

The Royal Air Force ultimately chose the belt-fed Browning for its aircraft instead, and surplus
Vickers K guns were passed on to British and Commonwealth ground units. There, the Long Range
Desert Group and the Special Air Service found its low-friction, gas-operated locking design
markedly more resistant to jamming from blown sand than either the water-cooled Vickers machine
gun or the Bren, and mounted pairs of them on their raiding vehicles in North Africa, where its
light weight and high rate of fire suited fast, aggressive hit-and-run tactics far better than its
original aerial role ever exploited.
