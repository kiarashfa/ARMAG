The AK-12 was designed at Izhmash, now Kalashnikov Concern, from 2011 and entered Russian
service production in 2018 as the newest formal successor to the AK-47 lineage, keeping the
same gas-operated, long-stroke piston, rotating-bolt action that has defined every Kalashnikov
rifle since 1947 while updating the furniture, sights and ergonomics around it. It replaces the
AK-74M and AK-100-series rifles as Russia's current-issue service rifle, retaining the AK
family's 5.45×39mm chambering while adding a folding, length-adjustable stock, an ambidextrous
fire selector and safety, and Picatinny rails for optics in place of the side-rail mounts used
on earlier Kalashnikov-pattern rifles.

Unlike most earlier Kalashnikov variants, which changed a proven action to fit a new
cartridge without otherwise redesigning it, the AK-12 was developed alongside a family of
siblings built on the same action but chambered for different cartridges entirely: the AK-15
in 7.62×39mm, the AK-19 in 5.56×45mm NATO for the export market, and the AK-308 in
7.62×51mm NATO. Because each of those changes the rifle's primary cartridge family rather
than only its furniture or sights, they are treated on this site as separate designs rather
than variants of this entry, even though they share the AK-12's action and much of its outer
architecture.

The AK-12 retains the Kalashnikov system's long-standing reputation for tolerance of dirt,
sand and poor maintenance, a design priority that traces back to its 1940s origins and that
the AK-12 program deliberately preserved rather than trading away for closer tolerances or
lighter construction. It has seen combat use in Syria and, since 2022, in Russia's invasion of
Ukraine, alongside older AK-74M and AK-100-series rifles still in wide Russian service.
