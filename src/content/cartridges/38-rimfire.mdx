The .38 rimfire (9.5×22mmRF) is a family of rimfire cartridges used in the United States from the mid-nineteenth century. They were made in short, long and extra-long lengths, and the .38 was preferred to the smaller .32 rimfire for hunting and self-defence because of its larger-diameter bullet and greater muzzle energy. It was a common cartridge for antique revolvers and rifles from the 1870s to the early 1900s, and through the 1890s a common self-defence round for a small revolver kept in a vest pocket.

Most revolvers and rifles were chambered for the Short or the Long; only a few rifles took the Extra Long. Shotshells also existed, and a few rolling-block, falling-block and bolt-action rifles had slightly choked smoothbore barrels to fire them at small game at close range. Hopkins & Allen made revolvers and rifles for it. Rifles chambered for it came from Remington (the revolving rifle of 1866), Ballard, Stevens and Frank Wesson, and revolvers from Enterprise, Favorite, Forehand & Wadsworth and Colt.

Production of rimfire calibres larger than .22 in the United States stopped when the country entered the Second World War and was not resumed by any major manufacturer, and factory ammunition now survives only as collectors' items.
