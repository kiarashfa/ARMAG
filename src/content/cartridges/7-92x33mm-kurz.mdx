name: "7.92×33mm Kurz"
---

The first intermediate cartridge to be issued in quantity, and the argument that every rifle
cartridge since has had to answer. German infantry studies in the 1930s established that almost all rifle
fire happened inside 400 m, and that the 7.92×57mm round every soldier carried was specified for four
times that. Polte's answer was to keep the bullet diameter and cut the case nearly in half.

The result gives up roughly half the muzzle energy of the full-power round and becomes controllable in
automatic fire from a shoulder weapon, which is the trade the assault rifle is built on. The Soviet
7.62×39mm followed it in 1943 and the American 5.56×45mm followed both, by a different route, twenty
years later.

Its name is the argument in miniature: Kurz simply means short, and what was shortened was the case of the
cartridge Germany had been issuing since 1888.
