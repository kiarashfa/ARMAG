---

The .50 GI is a rebated-rim pistol cartridge developed by Alex Zimmermann of Guncrafter Industries and introduced at the 2004 SHOT Show with the Guncrafter Industries Model No. 1, a 1911 variant. The rim is the same diameter as that of the .45 ACP, while the case body is wider and slightly longer. In 2006 Guncrafter added the Model No. 2, with a full-length light rail on the dust cover.

The bullet is 12.7 mm (.500 inch) in diameter and the case is rebated, meaning its rim is narrower than its body. The article says the Model No. 1 and No. 2 magazines hold seven rounds, and that a Glock 20 and 21 conversion upper holds eight in a standard magazine and nine with an extended base pad. The article lists the commercial handguns in the calibre as Guncrafter's own 1911 variants and its Glock conversion upper, with Magnum Research chambering the BFR revolver for it on a custom basis.

The article describes the round as built to recoil impulse and energy well below the magnum .50 handgun cartridges, the .50 Action Express and the .500 S&W Magnum, and says it has a reputation for accuracy, though that may reflect the precision of the pistols. It reports a 25-yard group of 2.56 inches for a 300-grain flat-point load and 2.3 inches for the 275-grain and 300-grain hollow-point loads.

Gaps on this page: C.I.P. has no sheet for this cartridge, so every dimension is the infobox's, converted from inches and recorded as estimated. The maximum pressure is left empty because the infobox (23,000 psi) and the article text (around 15,000 psi) disagree. The four loads record bullet mass only, because no test barrel is stated, and no ballistic coefficient is published.
