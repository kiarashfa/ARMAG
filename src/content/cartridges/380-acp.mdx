id: 380-acp
name: ".380 ACP"
---

The same bullet as the 9×19mm in a case two millimetres shorter, at two-thirds the pressure. That is
the entire design: Browning wanted a cartridge that a pistol could fire without a locked breech, and
135 MPa is about the ceiling at which a slide heavy enough to carry in a pocket can hold the case until the
bullet leaves.

The result is why so many small pistols are chambered for it. No locking lugs, no tilting barrel, no
linkage — a simple blowback with a fixed barrel is inherently more accurate and much cheaper to make. The
price is a sharp recoil impulse for the energy delivered, because the whole slide has to move.

Its name is a minor scandal of nomenclature: it is a nine-millimetre cartridge called .380, sold in Europe
as 9mm Browning court, 9mm Kurz and 9mm Corto, and C.I.P. files it under the French name.
