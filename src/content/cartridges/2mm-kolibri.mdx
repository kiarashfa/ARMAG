The 2mm Kolibri is the smallest commercially available centrefire cartridge, patented in 1910 and introduced in 1914 by Franz Pfannl, an Austrian watchmaker, with financial backing from Georg Grabner. It was designed for the Kolibri pistols, a semi-automatic and a single-shot version marketed as self-defence weapons. The name is the German word for hummingbird. It is also called the 2.7mm Kolibri Car Pistol or the 2.7×9mm Kolibri.

The round is rimless and straight-walled and measures 3.6 mm at its widest, 9.4 mm in case length and 11 mm overall, with a 2.7 mm bullet. The article says it headspaces on the mouth of the case, and that the whole cartridge has a mass of 5.3 grams (82 grains) and the bullet 0.2 grams (3 grains). Its estimated muzzle energy is 4 joules, which the article compares to less than a person's punch.

The article says the round was not well accepted: handling and loading single cartridges was difficult, and it records an accuracy problem, since the technology of the time could not rifle a bore of that size, so the bullet did not spin. The pistol was advertised as a ladies' self-defence weapon small enough to fit in a handbag, and production ended in 1914 with the outbreak of the First World War. The 2mm gun was replaced in the series by 3mm and 4mm weapons, and the series was discontinued in 1938. About a thousand of the firearms were made, and they are now collectors' items.
