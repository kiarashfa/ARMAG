The 7.65×25mm Borchardt was designed by Georg Johann Luger for Hugo Borchardt's Borchardt C-93 pistol, and the article calls it the first successful rimless pistol cartridge. The infobox dates the design to 1893. It has a rimless, bottlenecked case, and the article says it adapted features of the 7.92 mm cartridge of the 1888-pattern M/88 rifle, essentially scaling it down for a pistol.

Its importance is as a parent. The article says the Feederle brothers (Fidel, Friedrich and Josef) used it in their design for the Mauser C96, so that it was the basis of the 7.63×25mm Mauser, which used the same dimensions, and through that of the 7.62×25mm Tokarev. It was also the basis of the 7.65×21mm Parabellum and the 9×19mm Parabellum for the Luger pistol; the shorter case of the 7.65×21mm allowed a shorter toggle stroke and a smaller grip in the pistol, and the shorter length was kept for the 9×19mm. It was also the basis for the 7.65×25mm Mannlicher round of 1896 and 1903 and the 1902 self-loading carbine models, and was one of the chamberings of the Maxim-Silverman 1896 pistol.

The article says it was made by DWM in Germany, by Eley Brothers and Kynoch in Great Britain, and by Remington-UMC and Winchester in the United States, and that boxes were often marked as being for Borchardt and Mauser automatic pistols.
