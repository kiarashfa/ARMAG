The .460 S&W Magnum is a lengthened .454 Casull that Hornady and Smith & Wesson introduced in 2005 for
the Smith & Wesson Model 460, a revolver built for handgun hunting at longer range than earlier
big-bore revolver rounds allowed. Because the .454 Casull is itself a lengthened .45 Colt, all four
cartridges of the line share one bullet diameter.

That shared diameter runs in one direction only. A revolver chambered for the .460 will usually accept
the shorter .454 Casull, .45 Colt and .45 Schofield as well, though the maker of each firearm has to
confirm it, and lever-action rifles in particular feed only a limited range of lengths; the longer
.460 case will not enter a chamber cut for any of the three. Smith & Wesson describes the .460 as the
highest-velocity revolver cartridge in the world, a figure it reaches by pairing light-for-calibre
bullets with a large case and rifle-level pressure. Beyond revolvers, Big Horn Armory's Model 90 lever
action has been chambered for it, and the Ruger No. 1 and Thompson/Center Encore single-shot rifles
were once offered in it.

The dimensions and pressure ceiling on this page come from the C.I.P. datasheet for "460 S&W Mag.". The
one load recorded is Hornady factory ammunition with a velocity the maker states for an 8.375-inch test
barrel. No ballistic coefficient is recorded, and the encyclopedia's other listed loads come from
ammunition makers whose pages state no test barrel, so they are not recorded.
