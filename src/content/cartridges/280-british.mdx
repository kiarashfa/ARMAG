---
id: 280-british
name: ".280 British"
---

The British Army designed this cartridge after studying the German 7.92×33mm Kurz encountered during the
Second World War, an intermediate round sized between a pistol cartridge and a full-power rifle round that
let a shoulder-fired weapon fire automatically with controllable recoil. Britain set out to build its own
intermediate round with a longer effective range than the Kurz, and paired it with a purpose-built rifle,
the EM-2, alongside trials in the Lee-Enfield, the FN FAL and the Bren.

The round never reached service. Comparative testing against the American T65 cartridge at Aberdeen
Proving Ground in 1950 went against it — the US Army held that the .280 British did not meet its
requirements, citing accuracy and armour-piercing shortfalls — and a change of British government under
Winston Churchill, who prioritised cartridge commonality within NATO, ended the programme. NATO
standardised the American design as 7.62×51mm NATO instead.

No free dimensional standard exists for a cartridge that never reached production, so every figure in this
entry is converted from the reference work Cartridges of the World and carries `estimated` status. No
test-barrel length is published for either of its two loads, so only bullet mass is recorded for each.
