The .270 Winchester Short Magnum, or 270 WSM, is a rebated-rim bottlenecked cartridge Winchester introduced in 2002, made by necking the .300 Winchester Short Magnum down to hold a .277-inch bullet. It was one of the cartridges added that year to the Winchester Short Magnum family: the third commercial .270 ever produced, and the first in 60 years. SAAMI lists the name without a decimal point.

It improves on the older .270 Winchester: velocities are about 250 ft/s higher with bullets of the same weight, from a case short enough for a shorter action and a more compact rifle. It singles it out as the one WSM cartridge with notable ballistic gains over existing cartridges, and compares it to the .270 Weatherby Magnum, which needs a long action, usually with a 26-inch barrel against the WSM's 24-inch, and has a belt where the WSM does not.

The dimensions are C.I.P.'s: a 53.34 mm case, 72.64 mm overall length and a 440 MPa pressure ceiling. The one load recorded is a Hornady factory load, a 145-grain ELD-X at 3,100 ft/s from a 24-inch test barrel, the figure the page publishes.

Gaps on this page: Wikipedia's own ballistics table cites a handloading source, which publishes velocities per charge and so is not recorded; only the factory load above carries a velocity. Hornady's page gives no numeric ballistic coefficient, describing it only as best-in-class, so none is recorded.
