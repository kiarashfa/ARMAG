A gauge is not a calibre. The number counts how many spherical lead balls of bore diameter make up one
pound, so a larger number means a smaller bore, and twelve of them weigh sixteen ounces. That is why
this entry records a bore diameter and no bullet diameter: what leaves the muzzle may be nine hardened
pellets, a single slug or a sabot, and choosing one of those to call the projectile would be inventing
a fact about a cartridge that does not have one.

Shell length is the other trap. A shell described as 2-3/4 inch measures about 64 mm loaded and crimped
and opens to about 70 mm when fired, and it is the fired length the chamber has to accommodate. The two
numbers below are both recorded for that reason.

This entry has one load. The United States Army's M19 buckshot cartridge is fully documented in a
public-domain manual which gives its weight, its length, its pressure and its velocity, and does not
give the weight of the shot charge, so there is no honest way to enter it as a load here.
