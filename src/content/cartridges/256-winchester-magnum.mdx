The .256 Winchester Magnum is a rimmed, bottlenecked cartridge Winchester introduced in 1960 by necking the .357 Magnum case down to .257-inch diameter, for small game and varmints. The article lists Ruger and Winchester as designers. It was first chambered in the Ruger Hawkeye single-shot pistol, and in 1962 Marlin chambered its Model 62 Levermatic rifle for it. These were the two principal arms for the round; the article also lists a Universal Firearms carbine variant and Thompson/Center Contender barrels, and says Colt kept a prototype Python in this chambering in its museum.

The case shows its parent plainly: a rimmed case 32.54 mm long, with an 11.18 mm rim and a 9.68 mm base, necked to take a 6.53 mm bullet. The cartridge was used in both pistols and rifles, and Winchester advertised its 60-grain factory load at 2,350 ft/s from an 8.5-inch pistol barrel and at 2,760 ft/s from a 24-inch rifle barrel, and the article describes it as about 250 ft/s faster than the .22 Remington Jet, another cartridge built on a necked-down .357 Magnum case.

The article says the cartridge never achieved great commercial success. Winchester offered ammunition and reloading brass into the early 1990s, and it is now offered as a chambering only by custom barrelmakers.
