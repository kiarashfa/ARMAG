name: ".38 Super"
---

The .38 Super is a semi-rimmed, straight-walled pistol cartridge Colt introduced in 1929 as
a high-pressure development of the older .38 ACP, built to fit the same 1911-pattern frame.
Loaded well above the .38 ACP's operating pressure, it gave a full-size service pistol
meaningfully more muzzle energy than the .45 ACP standard of the day without changing the gun's
size or magazine capacity, and it became a fixture of USPSA/IPSC competition, where its
straight case and light bullets suit compensated race guns.

Dimensionally it sits between .38 ACP and 9×23mm Winchester: a 22.86 mm case, a 9.04 mm bullet,
and a 230 MPa C.I.P. pressure ceiling that is unusually high for a cartridge of this age, a
consequence of decades of loads built to run in modern, tightly headspaced 1911s rather than
the original 1929 pistols, which headspace on the semi-rimmed rim rather than the case mouth.

This entry carries the C.I.P. dimensional standard in full and one factory load, Federal's
American Eagle 115-grain jacketed hollow point rated at 344.4 m/s from a stated 5-inch test
barrel. No free source publishes a rifling twist rate for the cartridge's C.I.P. chamber
specifically for a commercial pistol barrel, and no photograph of a boxed cartridge or a
cutaway drawing was found under a usable licence — the hero image is a single loose round.
