The 7.35×51mm Carcano is a rimless, bottlenecked rifle cartridge the Italian military designed in the 1930s to replace the 6.5×52mm Carcano, which it never managed to do before 1945. It was a slightly modified 6.5×52mm case loaded with a 7.35 mm spitzer bullet, where the 6.5 mm round used a round-nosed one. The article says the first rifles chambered for it were issued in 1938.

The article's account of the background is that Italy chose its 6.5 mm cartridge with a round-nosed bullet in 1891, and kept it after most nations adopted spitzer bullets by 1908. Following complaints during the Second Italo-Abyssinian War about the ballistics of the 6.5 mm round in rifles and machine guns, Italy decided to adopt a larger bullet while keeping costs as low as possible. Experiments at the Terni Arms Factory, supervised by Colonel Giuseppe Mainardi, with Bombrini-Parodi-Delfino and Società Metallurgica Italiana, produced the 7.35 mm bullet. It is a 128-grain full-metal-jacketed lead-core spitzer with an aluminium tip, in a case using a Berdan primer.

With the outbreak of the Second World War the Italian government withdrew 7.35 mm weapons from frontline service and reissued the older 6.5 mm weapons, to relieve pressure on an overburdened logistics system. The remaining 7.35 mm weapons were issued to militia units or sold to Finland, which used them in the Continuation War. The article's see-also list pairs it with the 7.7×58mm Arisaka, adopted in the war for similar reasons.
