name: ".38-40 Winchester"
---

Winchester introduced the .38-40 in 1874, necking its own .44-40 case down to a bullet just over
10 mm in diameter to sit between that round and the smaller .32-20 in the same rimmed,
bottlenecked family. Despite the "38" in its name the bullet is actually .40 caliber — a
long-standing naming quirk of the black-powder era, when a cartridge's name described the powder
charge and bore loosely rather than a precise bullet diameter. Like its siblings it was built so a
Winchester lever rifle and a period revolver could share one box of ammunition.

Colt and other makers chambered revolvers for it specifically to pair with the Winchester 1873
and 1892 rifles, and the round stayed in production until 1937, by which point the dual-caliber
rifle-and-revolver arrangement it was built around had fallen out of fashion. It returned to
production in 1993, driven by the growth of cowboy action shooting, and modern loadings — including
specialist heavy loads for the stronger revolvers and rifles still chambered for it — are again
commercially available.

Case, bullet and pressure figures here are transcribed from the current C.I.P. datasheet and are
`verified`. Buffalo Bore's own product page for its 180-grain hard-cast load states two muzzle
velocities, one from a 5.5-inch revolver and one from a 24-inch rifle, both with the barrel
length named — which is what lets this entry carry two sourced velocity points for the same
bullet weight rather than the single, barrel-length-free figure that most other published sources
for this cartridge give.
