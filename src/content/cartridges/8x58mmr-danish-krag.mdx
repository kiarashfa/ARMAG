The 8×58mmR Danish Krag is a rimmed, bottlenecked centrefire military rifle cartridge of the late nineteenth century, developed in Denmark in 1888 by necking down the Danish 11.4×51mmR Remington rolling-block cartridge. Denmark adopted it the following year for the Krag–Jørgensen M89 rifle, and it stayed the standard Danish service rifle cartridge until 1945; Danish troops were still armed with it when Germany invaded in 1940. Denmark modernised the military load in 1908 with spitzer bullets.

It was briefly used elsewhere. Sweden carried out a major rebuilding of its M1867 rolling-block rifles for the cartridge, and Norway experimentally compared Remington rolling-block conversions in 8×58mmR with the Jarmann M1884. Service in Sweden was short, since Sweden and Norway developed the 6.5×55mm in 1891, approved it formally in 1893 and fielded it in 1894.

A similar name causes confusion: the Danish round must not be mixed up with the old German 8×58mmR, a different chambering. It also notes that surplus military rifles have been used for hunting, that ammunition was made in Otterup and by Norma Precision after the Second World War, and that firing modern loads in rifles lacking proof documentation involves uncertainty.
