The .17 Hornady Mach 2, usually written .17 HM2, is a rimfire cartridge Hornady introduced in 2004, following the 2002 launch of the .17 HMR. It is based on the .22 Long Rifle Stinger case necked down to .17 caliber (4.5 mm), with a bullet that weighs less than half what a typical .22 Long Rifle bullet does: 17 grains against 30 to 40.

The light bullet is the key to its high rimfire velocity, and whether the cartridge lives up to its Mach 2 name depends on location and conditions. It reaches 2,100 ft/s out of a rifle, nearly double a standard .22 LR, and has an effective range of 175 yards (160 m). The Hornady factory load recorded here is stated at 2,100 ft/s from a 24-inch test barrel.

Because the cartridge is based on the .22 LR, converting most bolt-action .22 LR rifles requires only a barrel change, while semi-automatic rifles are harder to convert since nearly all are blowback designs sensitive to higher chamber pressure; conversion kits fit a heavier bolt or bolt handle. CCI, Hornady and Eley make it.
