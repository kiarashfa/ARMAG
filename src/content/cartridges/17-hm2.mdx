The .17 Hornady Mach 2, usually written .17 HM2, is a rimfire cartridge Hornady introduced in 2004, following the 2002 launch of the .17 HMR. It is based on the .22 Long Rifle Stinger case necked down to .17 caliber (4.5 mm), with a bullet that weighs less than half what a typical .22 Long Rifle bullet does: 17 grains against 30 to 40.

The article says the light bullet is the key to high rimfire velocity, and that the cartridge may or may not live up to its Mach 2 name depending on location and conditions. It gives a velocity of 2,100 ft/s out of a rifle, nearly double a standard .22 LR, and an effective range of 175 yards (160 m). The Hornady factory load recorded here is stated at 2,100 ft/s from a 24-inch test barrel.

Because the cartridge is based on the .22 LR, the article says converting most bolt-action .22 LR rifles requires only a barrel change, while semi-automatic rifles are harder to convert since nearly all are blowback designs sensitive to higher chamber pressure; conversion kits fit a heavier bolt or bolt handle. The infobox lists CCI, Hornady and Eley as makers, and the .17 PMC/Aguila is in its see-also list.

Gaps on this page: C.I.P. has no sheet for the cartridge, so the dimensions and the pressure figure are the infobox's, uncited there, and recorded as estimated. Hornady's page gives no ballistic coefficient, so none is recorded.
