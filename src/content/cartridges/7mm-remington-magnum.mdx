---

The 7mm Remington Magnum reached the market in 1962, credited largely to hunting editor and outfitter Les
Bowman, who wanted a round with less recoil than the .300 Weatherby Magnum for clients hunting elk-sized
game at long range, while offering more retained energy at distance than the .270 Winchester managed with
the bullets available at the time. Bowman had used the .275 Holland & Holland Magnum abroad and found it a
good compromise, but it was never sold in the United States, so Remington built a belted magnum case along
similar lines for the American market instead.

Its arrival reshaped that market almost immediately: the existing .264 Winchester Magnum lost most of its
following within a year of the 7mm Remington Magnum's introduction, and Winchester answered in 1963 with
the .300 Winchester Magnum rather than trying to compete directly in 7mm. The two rival magnums that
emerged from that exchange, one from each company, have remained the most widely used magnum hunting
cartridges in North America ever since.

All dimensional and pressure figures on this page come from C.I.P.'s own published standard and are
recorded as verified; SAAMI, the body that governs it as an American commercial round, rates its pressure
ceiling somewhat lower under a different test method. Bullet mass and velocity for Hornady's 162-grain
ELD-X Precision Hunter load come from the manufacturer's own current product page and are likewise
verified. Two further bullet weights appear only in a handloading reference (Accurate Powder), whose
velocity figures reflect a specific propellant charge rather than the cartridge itself and are not recorded
here — only their bullet masses are.
