The 7.7×58mm Arisaka, formally the Type 99 7.7mm rimless, is a rimless bottlenecked rifle
cartridge adopted by the Imperial Japanese Army in 1939 for the Type 99 rifle and the Type 99
light machine gun. It replaced the earlier semi-rimmed 7.7×58mm Type 92, itself a rimmed
adaptation of the 6.5×50mm Arisaka's replacement programme, in Japan's move toward a rimless
cartridge more suited to belt-fed machine-gun use — a change driven directly by feeding problems
the semi-rimmed Type 92 round caused in automatic weapons.

Dimensionally it echoes the German 8×57mm IS that its designers studied closely, at a similar
case length and shoulder geometry but a narrower 7.92 mm bullet diameter matched to Japan's
existing 7.7 mm bore standard. Japan fielded three related but distinct 7.7 mm cartridges during
the Second World War — the semi-rimmed Type 92, the rimless Type 99, and a rimmed naval aircraft
variant — a proliferation that caused real wartime confusion between round and chamber.
