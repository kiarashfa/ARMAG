name: ".375 Ruger"
---

Ruger and Hornady introduced this cartridge in 2007 to close the gap between a standard-length bolt-action
and the .375 H&H Magnum, a dangerous-game round that had always needed a longer magnum-length action to
feed. Dropping the traditional belt let the case hold as much powder in a shorter body: the base is held
to the same diameter a belt would occupy, so the case gains the capacity a belt would otherwise have taken
up, while fitting Ruger's existing M77 rifle rather than requiring the longer M77 Safari Magnum action.

Its dimensions are set by a C.I.P. standard adopted in 2009, two years after the cartridge reached the
market, and every dimensional figure here is drawn from it. The three factory loads listed come from the
Wikipedia infobox without an inline citation to a Speer or Hornady datasheet of their own, so their bullet
masses and velocities are recorded as `estimated` rather than `verified`.

No ballistic-coefficient figure has been sourced for any of its three loads.
