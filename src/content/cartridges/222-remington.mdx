The .222 Remington is a rimless, bottlenecked rifle cartridge Remington introduced in 1950 as the
first commercial rimless .22-caliber cartridge made in the United States. Its accuracy and flat
trajectory led to its adoption for varmint and benchrest rifles.

In 1963 a stretched version of the .222 case, the .222 Remington Special, was standardised
alongside the new M16 rifle as the .223 Remington and 5.56×45mm NATO; the .223 Remington and
5.56×45mm NATO entries on this site have the fuller account of that later, larger-scale military
development. The original .222 Remington itself was never adopted as a military round and has
remained a civilian sporting cartridge throughout its production life.

All dimensional and pressure figures on this page come from C.I.P.'s published standard and are
recorded as verified. Four bullet weights are recorded from a handloading data page cited by
Wikipedia's infobox, but that page's published velocities are maximum-handload results tied to a
specific powder charge rather than factory-ammunition figures, so no velocity or energy is
recorded for this cartridge. No photograph of a rifle chambered for it appears on this site.
