The .300 Winchester Short Magnum is a rebated-rim, bottlenecked rifle cartridge Winchester
introduced in 2001, built to deliver .300 Winchester Magnum-class performance from a case short
enough to feed through a standard-length rifle action rather than the long magnum action the
older cartridge needs. Its case is unbelted and its rim narrower than its body — the rebated-rim
design shared across the whole WSM family — which lets a fat, short case headspace and feed
through a bolt face sized for a standard .30-06-length cartridge.

At 53.34 mm case length against the .300 Winchester Magnum's 66 mm, the WSM trades a longer
powder column for a wider one, aiming to reach comparable velocities with less overall cartridge
length and, Winchester argued, better powder-burn efficiency. Its 440 MPa C.I.P. pressure ceiling
is high for a commercial hunting round, reflecting the shorter case's smaller powder charge
working at higher pressure to reach magnum velocities.

This entry carries the full C.I.P. dimensional standard and one factory load, Hornady's Precision
Hunter 200-grain ELD-X rated at 859.5 m/s from a stated 24-inch test barrel. No ballistic
coefficient is published on that product page, so the cartridge computes a muzzle energy figure
but no trajectory. No twist rate specific to a commercial .300 WSM barrel is recorded here, only
the general 1-in-10-inch figure that recurs across sporting-rifle listings for the cartridge.
