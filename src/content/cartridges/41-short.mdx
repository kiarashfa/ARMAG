The .41 Short Rimfire, usually called the .41 Short, is a rimmed, straight-walled rimfire cartridge first introduced by the National Arms Company in 1863. It was created for use in a small single-shot derringer, which probably explains its very low ballistics, and Remington began producing its over-under Model 95 derringer in .41 Short Rimfire in 1866. In 1873 the slightly more powerful .41 Long (rimfire) and the .41 Short Colt (a centrefire cartridge) were introduced in the Colt New Line pocket revolver. This entry covers the rimfire round, not the centrefire Colt cartridge.

Cartridges of the World gives a 130-grain lead bullet at 425 ft/s and 52 foot-pounds of energy, but the firearms writer Holt Bodinson measured 621 ft/s and 111 foot-pounds, a large difference attributed to variation in ammunition or measuring equipment. It also reports a test with a classic double-barrelled derringer with 3-inch barrels, in which two types of cartridge were fired and velocity was measured 10 feet from the muzzle: an older Western Lubaloy load averaged 532 ft/s and a newer Navy Arms batch 621 ft/s.
