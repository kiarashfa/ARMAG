The .444 Marlin is a semi-rimmed, straight-walled rifle cartridge Marlin Firearms and Remington
Arms co-developed in 1964 for the Marlin Model 444 lever-action rifle, built to give lever-gun
shooters a cartridge with more reach and retained energy than the .30-30 Winchester and .35
Remington then dominant in that class. Its case is essentially a lengthened .44 Magnum, sharing
that round's 10.93 mm bullet diameter but built for a rifle-length action and a substantially
higher pressure ceiling.

At a 355 MPa C.I.P. pressure ceiling, it sits well above typical lever-action straight-wall
cartridges of its era, trading some of a bottlenecked case's efficiency for the simple, rimless
feeding a tubular magazine and a lever action's angled bolt face both need. It remains a
dedicated big-game round for the same short-to-medium-range lever-action niche it was designed
for, now most often loaded with polymer-tipped spitzer-profile bullets safe to stack nose-to-primer
in a tubular magazine.

This entry carries the full C.I.P. dimensional standard and one current factory load, Hornady's
LEVERevolution 265-grain FTX rated at 708.7 m/s from a stated 24-inch test barrel. No ballistic
coefficient is published on that product page, so the cartridge computes a muzzle energy figure
but no trajectory.
