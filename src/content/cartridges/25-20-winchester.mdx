The .25-20 Winchester (.25-20 WCF, 6.6×33mmR) is a rimmed, bottlenecked cartridge made by necking down the .32-20 Winchester. It was developed around 1895 for the Winchester Model 1892 lever-action rifle, although Wikipedia's infobox dates the design to 1892. Two years earlier Marlin had necked down the .32-20 and called the result the .25-20 Marlin, chambering it in the Model 1889 lever-action rifle long before Winchester put its name on the cartridge.

In the early twentieth century it was a popular small game and varmint round, once a favourite with farmers, ranchers and trappers, and it remains a viable small game and fur-bearer cartridge. It is sometimes confused with the .25-20 Single Shot, a markedly different cartridge that does not interchange with it. The SAAMI pressure rating is 28,000 CUP, but modern ammunition is often loaded lighter out of consideration for the weaker steels in many original guns.

Gaps on this page: case dimensions and the pressure ceiling are C.I.P.'s and verified. Wikipedia's velocities come from a handloading guide and a loading manual, so none is recorded, and the three loads are bullet masses only. No factory-load velocity, ballistic coefficient or designer is published, and the 1895 introduction year is from Wikipedia's text, with its infobox giving 1892 for the design.
