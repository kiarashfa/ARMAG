---
id: 45-colt
name: ".45 Colt"
---

Adopted with the Single Action Army in 1873 and the US Army's handgun cartridge for the next two
decades. It began as a black-powder round carrying a 250-grain lead bullet, and the
case is long because black powder is bulky, not because the round is powerful.

Its rim is unusually small for the case head, a detail that mattered once revolvers began ejecting all six
chambers at once: the extractor star has little to grip. Smith & Wesson's shorter .45 Schofield had a
larger rim and would chamber in a Colt, which is why the Army spent years issuing two incompatible
cartridges to units carrying two incompatible revolvers.

The C.I.P. ceiling of 110 MPa reflects the antique arms still chambered for it. Modern revolvers built on
heavier frames are loaded far beyond it, which is a fact about those revolvers rather than about this
standard.
