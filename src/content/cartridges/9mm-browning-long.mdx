The 9mm Browning Long (9×20mmSR) is a semi-rimmed, straight-walled military pistol cartridge John Browning developed in 1903 for the FN Model 1903, the pistol adopted by Belgium, France, Estonia, the Netherlands and Sweden. It is similar to the 9×19mm Parabellum but has a slightly longer case and headspaces on the rim.

The article says FN developed it for the blowback-operated Model 1903, a pistol using the same Browning patent as the Colt Model 1903 Pocket Hammerless, and that a more powerful cartridge such as the 9×19mm Parabellum would have required a locked-breech design. C.I.P. gives a 165 MPa pressure ceiling, far below the 440 MPa it gives for the 9 mm Luger, which is consistent with that blowback pairing. Ammunition was made in Belgium, France, England, Sweden and the United States, with some production in Germany during the First World War for the Ottoman Empire, and the cartridge was also used in South Africa.

The article calls the cartridge obsolete, says reloadable brass is hard to find and that handloaders can shorten .38 Super cases, and names Prvi Partizan in Serbia and the Mechanical and Chemical Industry Corporation of Ankara as current makers. A Swedish box in the gallery shows the round as Sweden issued it, as the m/07.

Gaps on this page: case dimensions and the pressure ceiling are C.I.P.'s and verified. Both loads carry bullet mass only, from the encyclopedia and uncited there, because no test barrel is stated for their velocities. The article's handloading figures are not recorded, and no ballistic coefficient is published.
