The .351 Winchester Self-Loading is a straight-walled, semi-rimmed rifle cartridge that Winchester designed in 1906 for its Model 1907 self-loading rifle, as a replacement for the Model 1905 and its .35 Winchester Self-Loading round. It was the only chambering offered in the Model 1907. It proved popular with police and security forces and was used by France in both world wars, and the 8×35mm cartridge of the Ribeyrolles M1918 was based on it. An experimental Thompson submachine gun was also made for it in 1919 but never sold commercially.

Its distinguishing dimension is the bullet: an unusual diameter of .351 inch instead of the .357 or .358 more common in .35-caliber rifle rounds. C.I.P. gives 8.94 mm, which matches. The case is only 35.05 mm long and the loaded round 48.26 mm, so the cartridge sits between a pistol and a rifle round in size, and C.I.P. gives its pressure ceiling as 365 MPa.

Most commercial loads launched a 180-grain bullet at 1,850 to 1,925 ft/s from a 20-inch barrel, comparable to .30-30 Winchester and .35 Remington loads fired from barrels of the same length. Today's ammunition comes from a few small makers, often with cast lead or plated bullets or resized .358 jacketed bullets, and is frequently loaded to lower velocities out of concern for old rifles that have not been maintained.

Gaps on this page: case dimensions and the pressure ceiling are C.I.P.'s and verified. The one load, a 180-grain bullet at 570 m/s from a 508 mm barrel, is taken from the encyclopedia's infobox, which cites a 1918 book rather than a linked source, so it is estimated. No ballistic coefficient is published. No factory-load page survives for the original ammunition.
