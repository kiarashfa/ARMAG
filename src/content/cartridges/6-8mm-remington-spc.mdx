name: "6.8mm Remington SPC"
---

The US Special Operations Command worked with Remington Arms and the US Army Marksmanship Unit between
2002 and 2004 to develop a cartridge whose downrange ballistics held up better than 5.56×45mm NATO's when
fired from the short barrels common on carbines, while still fitting the magazine and bolt-face dimensions
of the existing M16 rifle family and keeping recoil close to what a 5.56mm carbine already produced. The
case is based on the older .30 Remington round, shortened and necked down to a .277 bullet so it would
feed from a modified AR-15 magazine.

Its dimensions are governed by a C.I.P. standard adopted in 2006, two years after the cartridge's
introduction, and every dimensional figure here comes from that standard. The four load rows are factory
ammunition figures the cited Wikipedia article attributes to SSA and Hornady product listings rather than
to a document consulted directly here, so they are recorded as `estimated` pending an independent check
of each manufacturer's own page.

No ballistic-coefficient figure has been sourced for any of its four loads.
