The 9×21mm Gyurza is a Russian military round TsNIITochMash developed through the 1990s to defeat
body armour at handgun ranges, entirely unrelated to the Italian/IMI 9×21mm commercial round of the
same designation despite sharing a case length and a rimless straight case shape by coincidence
rather than lineage. It was built for the Serdyukov SPS pistol, later marketed as the SR-1 Vektor,
and for the Gyurza-2/SR-1M and Udav pistols that followed it, giving Russian forces a sidearm round
credited with defeating soft body armour and light steel plate at short range — the design brief the
9×21 case was built around, rather than a claim about the round's effect on a person.

The family carries several loadings under Russian military nomenclature: SP-10 (index 7N29) with an
armour-piercing steel core, SP-11 (7N28) with a conventional jacketed bullet, and SP-13 (7BTZ) an
expanding bullet intended for use against unprotected targets. The predecessor round shown in this
entry's photograph, the 9×21 RG054, is a 1994 pre-series cartridge rather than one of the
standardised production loadings.

No free dimensional standard exists for this cartridge — it has no C.I.P. or SAAMI listing — so every
dimension here is recorded as estimated from Wikipedia's own uncited infobox figures. The three
loads' bullet masses are similarly estimated from the same infobox, which in turn cites the
now-defunct world.guns.ru rather than a primary Russian source; its successor site,
modernfirearms.net, states a 420 m/s muzzle velocity for the SR-1 Vektor pistol but no barrel
length, so no velocity or ballistic coefficient is recorded for any load. No image of a production
SP-10 or SP-11 round, rather than this pre-series RG054 predecessor, turned up on Wikimedia Commons,
Smithsonian Open Access or DVIDS.
