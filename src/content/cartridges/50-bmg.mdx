John Browning scaled his .30-06 up by a factor of roughly four in 1918, after watching German
anti-tank rifles and the first armoured aircraft, and the result has outlasted every vehicle it was
designed to defeat. A 45 g bullet leaving a metre-long barrel at more than 900 m/s carries about
18,000 joules — an order of magnitude past a rifle cartridge, which is why the arms that fire it weigh
what they weigh.

It is also the clearest case on this site of a cartridge whose specification is not public. C.I.P. does
not list it. The NATO standard costs money. SAAMI publishes a drawing that cannot be redistributed. Every
dimension below is therefore the encyclopedia's figure, labelled as an estimate, and no load row appears
at all — the freely available velocity tables are handloading data from commercial reloading sites, which
is neither a standard nor something this site records.
