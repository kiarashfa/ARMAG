name: ".38-55 Winchester"

The .38-55 is a straight-walled, rimmed centrefire rifle cartridge based on the earlier .38-50 Ballard Everlasting, introduced in 1876 by the Ballard Rifle & Cartridge Company. As the .38-55 Ballard it was first chambered by Ballard and Marlin for single-shot target rifles. Winchester later offered the cartridge and renamed it the .38-55 Winchester, as the first chambering of its Model 1894. It was identical to the Ballard round except that it was loaded for smokeless propellant.

The name follows the black-powder-era convention of pairing bore diameter with a second number that described the original loading of the thin-walled, folded-head case rather than any dimension of the cartridge, and the designation stayed after the case was thickened so that it could be reloaded. Marlin's 1893 lever-action rifle was offered with a barrel for black-powder loads only and another marked for smokeless loads, and Marlin later chambered some Model 336 rifles for it. Winchester used the round in various rifles until about 1940, and it also appeared in the Remington-Lee bolt-action rifle.

Its main legacy is as a parent case: the article names it as the parent of the .30-30 Winchester, the .32 Winchester Special and the .375 Winchester. The .375 Winchester of 1978 has similar brass, about a millimetre shorter, but was designed for higher pressures and modern arms only, and the article warns that its factory ammunition is not safe in older .38-55 rifles. The article says the cartridge is used to hunt black bear and deer at moderate ranges and in cowboy action shooting side matches.
