The .454 Casull is a lengthened and strengthened .45 Colt, worked up as a wildcat in 1958 by Dick
Casull, Duane Marsh and Jack Fullmer and announced by Guns & Ammo magazine in November 1959. It stayed
a wildcat for more than two decades, until Freedom Arms brought a five-shot single-action revolver
chambered for it to market in 1983; SAAMI published its first standard for the round in 1997.

The longer case keeps the .45 Colt's bullet diameter, which sets the family relationship in one
direction only: a .454 Casull chamber also accepts the shorter .45 Colt and .45 Schofield, while the
Casull round will not enter a revolver built for either of them. It runs at rifle-level pressure and
uses a small rifle primer rather than a pistol primer for that reason. Ruger chambered its Super
Redhawk for the round in 1997 and Taurus its Raging Bull in 1998, and the cartridge has been used
chiefly for hunting medium and large game, for metallic silhouette shooting and as a revolver carried
for protection against bears. The .460 S&W Magnum of 2005 is in turn a lengthened .454 Casull.

Every dimension and the pressure ceiling on this page come from the C.I.P. datasheet for "454
Casull". Both loads are Hornady factory ammunition, each with a muzzle velocity the maker states for a
7.5-inch barrel. No ballistic coefficient is recorded for either, so no trajectory beyond the muzzle
can be computed for this cartridge.
