name: ".44 Special"
---

The .44 Special is a rimmed, straight-walled revolver cartridge Smith & Wesson introduced in
1907 as a longer, higher-pressure development of the .44 Russian, built for the New Century
"Triple Lock" revolver. It shares its 10.98 mm bullet diameter with the .44 Russian and, decades
later, the .44 Magnum, which Remington and Smith & Wesson created in 1955 simply by lengthening
the .44 Special case and loading it to far higher pressure — the .44 Special still chambers and
fires safely in any .44 Magnum revolver, though the reverse is not true.

At a 100 MPa C.I.P. pressure ceiling, roughly a fifth of the .44 Magnum's, it is a comparatively
mild round for its bullet diameter, which has kept it in production for target shooting, and for
compact carry revolvers such as the Charter Arms Bulldog, where the shorter case and lower
pressure suit a small, lightweight frame the full Magnum load would not.

This entry carries the full C.I.P. dimensional standard and one factory load, Federal's Champion
Training 200-grain semi-wadcutter hollow point rated at 265.2 m/s from a stated 4-inch vented
test barrel. No ballistic coefficient is published for that bullet, so the cartridge computes a
muzzle energy figure but no trajectory, and no rifling twist specific to a commercial .44 Special
revolver barrel was found published free.
