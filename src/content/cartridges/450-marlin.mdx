The .450 Marlin was designed as a modernised equivalent of the .45-70 by a joint team of Marlin and
Hornady engineers led by Hornady's Mitch Mittelstaedt, and released in 2000, with Hornady making the
ammunition and Marlin the rifle, mainly the Model 1895M lever action. The Browning BLR and the Ruger
No. 1 have since been chambered for it; Marlin stopped making the 1895M in 2009.

Ballistically it is close to the .45-70, but it was not developed from it. Its parent is the
.458×2-inch American wildcat, itself based on the .458 Winchester Magnum, which puts the .450 Marlin
in the belted .458 Winchester family. A .45-70 can be handloaded to pressures that would destroy an old
rifle such as the Trapdoor Springfield; the .450 Marlin offers the ballistics of such loads with no
risk of being chambered in one. Its belt was modified so that it will not chamber in smaller-bore 7 mm
or .338 magnum rifles, and the .45-70 and the .450 Marlin cannot be cross-chambered. The case also
suits a bolt action, though only Steyr-Mannlicher built one for it; other gunsmiths have converted
existing bolt rifles.

Every dimension in this entry comes from the C.I.P. datasheet for "450 Marlin". The Hornady 325-grain
FTX load is taken from Hornady's own product page, which states its 24-inch test barrel and a G1 ballistic
coefficient of .230. Wikipedia's infobox names three further loads with the same stated test barrel, but
their cited product pages could not be reconfirmed live (Buffalo Bore's URL now serves a reorganised
catalogue), so those three are recorded as estimated, though each velocity reproduces its own published
energy figure through ½mv² almost exactly. No ballistic coefficient is published for them.
