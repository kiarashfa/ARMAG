---

The .476 Enfield, formally the .476 Enfield Mark III, was the British Army's standard revolver
cartridge from 1881 to 1891, chambered in the Enfield Mk II break-top revolver and named for the
service that adopted it rather than for a manufacturer. It fires a large, heavy lead bullet at
modest velocity from a short, straight-walled rimmed case, in keeping with the era's preference for
bullet mass and diameter over speed in a service handgun.

It was superseded from 1891 by the .455 Webley, later standardised as .455 Enfield, a cartridge
close enough in bullet diameter that the two are sometimes confused; despite the similar name, the
.476 Enfield's slightly larger rim and base diameters mean the two rounds are not directly
interchangeable in a revolver chambered for one or the other. Colonial military forces and Canada's
North-West Mounted Police carried the .476 Enfield alongside the British Army during its period of
issue.

No free dimensional standard exists for this cartridge — it predates the modern C.I.P. and SAAMI
bodies entirely — so every dimension in this entry is recorded as estimated from Wikipedia's own
infobox, which cites a 1972 print reference book rather than a source that can carry a `verified`
status. No load with a bullet mass, velocity or energy figure is recorded on Wikipedia at all, so
none appears in this entry.
