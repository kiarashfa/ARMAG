The 8×50mmR Mannlicher, adopted by Austria-Hungary in 1890 as the M. 90, is a rimmed, bottlenecked
rifle cartridge converted from the earlier 8×52mmR Mannlicher round. It armed the Austro-Hungarian
Mannlicher M1895 straight-pull rifle and carbine, fed from Mannlicher-pattern en-bloc clips that
loaded five rounds into the rifle's magazine at once and ejected automatically once empty.

The cartridge remained Austria-Hungary's standard rifle round through the First World War, and
surplus rifles and ammunition in the calibre saw further service with Bulgaria and Italy, including
use by Italy in the Second World War. It was later replaced by the 8×56mmR, for which many of the
rifles were rechambered.

Case and pressure dimensions here come from C.I.P.'s published standard and are recorded as
verified. Two historical ball loads are recorded from Wikipedia's infobox, which carries no
further citation for either bullet mass or velocity figure; both are therefore recorded as
estimated rather than verified. No photograph of an M1895 rifle chambered for this round appears
