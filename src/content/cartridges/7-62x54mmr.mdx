Adopted with the Mosin-Nagant in 1891 and still in front-line service, this is the longest-serving
rifle cartridge in the world and the last rimmed military round of consequence. The rim was not a
conservative choice at the time; it was the ordinary one, and it survived because Soviet designers kept
finding ways to work around it rather than replacing the ammunition stockpile. A rimmed case will not
stack neatly in a box magazine and will not push straight forward out of a belt, which is why the
machine guns that use it strip the round backwards out of the belt before chambering it.

The dimensions here are C.I.P. cartridge maxima. Wartime and post-war Soviet production varied
considerably around them, and any figure for a specific arsenal lot is not something this page claims.
