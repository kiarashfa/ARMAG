The .30-40 Krag, originally designated the .30 U.S. or .30 Army, is a rimmed, bottlenecked rifle
cartridge adopted by the United States in 1892 for the Krag–Jørgensen rifle. It was the first
smokeless-powder cartridge issued by the US military, replacing the black-powder .45-70 Government
and its trapdoor Springfield, and it saw combat in the Spanish–American War before the US Army
replaced both rifle and cartridge with the M1903 Springfield and the rimless .30-03 in the early
1900s.

Its name follows the black-powder-era convention of pairing bore diameter with a second number —
here 40, for the original 40-grain smokeless charge, not a black-powder weight — a naming quirk
this site records as a fact about how the cartridge got its name rather than as a stated load. At
a 325 MPa C.I.P. pressure ceiling and a rimmed, single-locking-lug action behind it, the Krag rifle
could not be loaded as fast from a magazine as its rimless European contemporaries, one of the
technical shortcomings that pushed the US Army toward the rimless Mauser-influenced M1903 within
a decade.

This entry carries the full C.I.P. dimensional standard and one current factory load, Hornady's
Custom 180-grain spire point rated at 740.8 m/s from a stated 24-inch test barrel — this reflects
a modern sporting-rifle loading, not the original 1892 military charge. No ballistic coefficient
is published on that product page, so the cartridge computes a muzzle energy figure but no
trajectory.
