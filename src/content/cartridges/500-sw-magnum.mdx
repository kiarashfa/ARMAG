The .500 S&W Magnum is a semi-rimmed .50-calibre revolver cartridge developed by Cor-Bon with Smith &
Wesson's "X-Gun" engineering team for the Model 500 X-frame revolver, both unveiled early in 2003. It
was designed from the outset to be the most powerful production handgun cartridge, a title Smith &
Wesson had held with the .44 Magnum until the .454 Casull overtook it. S&W product manager Herb Belin
proposed the revolver and its cartridge to the company's sales team, and the project ran for eleven
months from approval to unveiling.

The case is a straight cylinder that headspaces on its rim, but the rim is only slightly larger than
the body, which lets the round cycle more smoothly through tubular magazines than a fully rimmed
revolver cartridge; it does not feed well from a box magazine, where the rim tends to lock into the
extractor groove of the round beneath. Its bullet is 0.500 inch across, smaller than the 0.510 inch of
the earlier .500 Linebaugh. Cor-Bon later brought out a shorter .500 S&W Special for milder shooting
in the same revolvers, and Taurus chambered its Raging Bull for the full-length round for a time.

The dimensions and pressure ceiling on this page come from the C.I.P. datasheet for "500 S&W Mag.".
Both loads are Hornady factory ammunition, spanning a light 300-grain bullet and a heavy 500-grain one,
each with a velocity the maker states for an 8.5-inch test barrel. No ballistic coefficient is
recorded for either.
