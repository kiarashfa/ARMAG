The 7.65×21mm Parabellum is Georg Luger's 1898 reduction of Hugo Borchardt's earlier
7.65×25mm round to fit the toggle-lock pistol Luger was developing for DWM, the direct
ancestor of what later became the 9×19mm Parabellum. It was the original chambering of the
Luger pistol and, under its American name .30 Luger, remained available alongside the larger
9mm round throughout the Luger's production life. Switzerland adopted it for a domestic Luger
variant and kept it in service into the mid-20th century, and it also saw issue or trial use in
Germany, Portugal, Brazil, Finland and, in small numbers, the United States.

Bottlenecked and rimless, it was among the first successful smokeless pistol cartridges of its
kind and shares its case head dimensions closely with the later 7.62×25mm Tokarev, though the two
are not interchangeable. Its light, fast bullet gives it a flatter trajectory than most contemporary
pistol cartridges of similar bore, and a lower bullet mass and muzzle energy than the 9×19mm that
was developed from it by opening the case mouth out to take a wider bullet. It fell out of military and police use after the Second World War as the
9×19mm and other cartridges took over Luger-pattern and successor pistols, and today it survives
mainly as a collector's and vintage-firearm round.

Every dimension in this entry comes from the C.I.P. datasheet for "7,65 Parabellum", which matches
Wikipedia's own infobox figures exactly. Fiocchi still loads a modern factory round for collectors
under its Heritage line, and its product page states a bullet mass of 93 grains but no test barrel
length for its stated velocity, so only the bullet mass is recorded here; no velocity or ballistic
coefficient is recorded for this cartridge.
