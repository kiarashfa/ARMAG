The .32 S&W Long is a rimmed, straight-walled revolver cartridge Smith & Wesson introduced in
1896 by lengthening its own earlier .32 S&W case. Colt chambered the same round in its own
revolvers under the name .32 Colt New Police, and both names describe the identical cartridge
rather than two different rounds.

The round was widely chambered in small- and medium-frame pocket and police revolvers from both
makers through the first half of the twentieth century, valued for its light recoil compared to
larger service revolver cartridges of the period. It remained in production for target shooting
and small-frame defensive revolvers, and its case later formed the basis for the longer, higher-
pressure .32 H&R Magnum.

All dimensional and pressure figures on this page come from C.I.P.'s published standard and are
recorded as verified. Three bullet weights are recorded from a handloading data page cited by
Wikipedia's infobox, but that page's published velocities are maximum-handload results tied to a
specific powder charge rather than factory-ammunition figures, so no velocity or energy is
recorded for this cartridge. No photograph of a revolver chambered for it appears on this site.
