China spent eight years from 1979 developing a cartridge to replace both the 7.62×39mm and the
7.62×54mmR in one calibre, and the 5.8×42mm is the only wholly new service rifle round any major power has
fielded since the 1970s. Its specification was written against both Western and Soviet small-calibre rounds:
a heavier bullet than either at a similar velocity, on the argument that retained energy at 600 m matters
more than muzzle figures.

Two loadings existed for a decade because one cartridge could not do both jobs — the lighter DBP87 for the
rifle and the heavier DBP88 for machine guns and marksman rifles — and the DBP10 of 2010 was an attempt to
replace both with a single round.

No standards body publishes it. C.I.P. lists no 5.8 mm cartridge at all, the Chinese national defence
standard is not free, and every dimension on this page is therefore an encyclopedia figure recorded as an
estimate. The three load rows are better founded: the article states the 557 mm test barrel that produced
them, and ½mv² reproduces its published energies exactly.
