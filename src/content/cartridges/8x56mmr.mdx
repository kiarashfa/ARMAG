The 8×56mmR is a rimmed, bottlenecked rifle cartridge that Austria adopted in 1930, Hungary in 1931 and Bulgaria in 1934 as a replacement for the 8×50mmR Mannlicher. It was created to update the Steyr-Mannlicher M1895 rifle: the barrel was shortened and the chamber recut for the new round, and Hungary then chose it for its 35M rifle as well.

The cartridge also went into updated machine guns of the period, among them the Solothurn 31M and the Schwarzlose 07/31M, and from 1934 it was Bulgaria's standard military cartridge. Ammunition was made in Austria, Germany, Bulgaria and Czechoslovakia. It is no longer in use by any organised military; Hornady and Prvi Partizan are current commercial makers.

The dimensions explain why it is difficult to handload. C.I.P. gives a bullet diameter of 8.4 mm, wider than the 8.22 mm of the 8×50mmR it replaced, and the groove diameter is .329 inch, so the common .323-inch 8 mm bullets group poorly. Hornady makes a .330-inch bullet for the round and Prvi Partizan loads 208-grain bullets of that size. 7.62×54mmR cases can be reformed to this one, leaving a neck about 2 mm short that still headspaces on the rim, because the round is a rimmed design.

Gaps on this page: case dimensions and the pressure ceiling are C.I.P.'s and are recorded as verified. The two bullet masses come from the encyclopedia's table, which cites a C.I.P. page that does not print bullet masses, so both are estimated. That table lists velocities but no test barrel, so no velocity is recorded for either load, and no ballistic coefficient is published. The introduction year is that of the first adoption, by Austria.
