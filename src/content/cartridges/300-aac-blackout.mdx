The .300 AAC Blackout was developed by Advanced Armament Corporation and introduced in 2010 to
solve a problem specific to the AR-15/M4 platform: fitting a 7.62 mm-class bullet into a rifle built
around a 5.56x45mm bolt, magazine and bolt carrier, with no parts swap beyond the barrel. Earlier
attempts at the same idea, including AAC's own .300 Whisper wildcat, are effectively the same case;
the Blackout is the SAAMI- and C.I.P.-standardised version of that idea.

The case is a shortened and blown-out .223 Remington, cut back to 34.75 mm and opened up to take a
7.85 mm bullet, with a C.I.P. overall length limit of 57.4 mm — short enough to feed from an
unmodified 5.56x45mm magazine and to headspace on an unmodified bolt face. That constraint is also
what makes the round unusual: with the same case capacity as a 5.56x45mm round but a much larger,
heavier bullet, it is loaded two different ways for two different jobs. Light, fast bullets around
110–125 grains give supersonic performance broadly comparable to 7.62x39mm from a short barrel;
heavy 220-grain bullets loaded to stay below the speed of sound are quiet enough, especially
suppressed, to be the round's other major use case, one 5.56x45mm cannot match without a different
bolt and magazine.
