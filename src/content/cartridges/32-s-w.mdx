The .32 S&W, also known as the .32 S&W Short, is a rimmed, straight-walled revolver cartridge the
Union Metallic Cartridge Company designed and Smith & Wesson introduced in 1878 for its pocket
revolvers. It was offered to the public as a light defensive cartridge for very short distances, and
it remained popular in the United States and Europe long after the revolvers made for it were out of
production.

It was the basis for several later .32-calibre handgun cartridges, among them the .32 S&W Long, the
.32 H&R Magnum and the .327 Federal Magnum, and it can be fired safely in revolvers chambered for
any of the three.

All dimensional and pressure figures on this page come from C.I.P.'s published standard and are
recorded as verified. Two bullet weights are recorded from a published loading reference cited by
Wikipedia's infobox, but no test-barrel length is stated for either load's velocity, so no
velocity or energy is recorded for this cartridge. No photograph of a revolver chambered for it
appears on this site.
