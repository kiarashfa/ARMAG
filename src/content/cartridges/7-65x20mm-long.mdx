The 7.65×20mm Long was designed at Remington Arms in 1917 by John Pedersen as a compact,
rimless pistol-and-carbine round intended for the semi-automatic Pedersen device, an insert
that let a bolt-action Springfield rifle fire it as a semi-automatic carbine. The device never
reached combat before the First World War ended, but France adopted the cartridge afterward
for its own service pistols and submachine guns, and it remained in French service use into
the 1960s under the name 7.65mm Longue.

Its straight, rimless case and small overall length reflect its origin as a compact
intermediate round rather than a full-power rifle cartridge: it was built to give a pistol- or
carbine-length action worthwhile range and accuracy without the size or recoil of a full
rifle round, sitting between contemporary pistol cartridges and full-power rifle rounds of the
period. France chambered it in service pistols such as the MAS Modèle 1935 series and in
submachine guns including the MAS-38.

This entry's dimensions come from Wikipedia's infobox, none of them individually cited on

military round — it never went into commercial production outside France's own service
