The 9×53mmR is a rimmed, bottlenecked rifle cartridge M. N. Blum designed in 1955 by necking up
the Soviet 7.62×54mmR service case to take a much larger, 9.27 mm bullet. It was built as a
civilian hunting round rather than a military one, drawing on a rimmed case already produced at
scale for the Mosin–Nagant family of rifles and machine guns.

Production began in 1962 and continued, with a gap from 1992 to 1997, into the post-Soviet
Russian ammunition industry. Since 1971 the round has been loaded in two standard bullet types
for different game classes: a soft-point load for larger animals and a full-metal-jacket load for
smaller ones. Several Soviet- and Russian-made hunting rifles have been chambered for it,
including bolt-action carbines and, more recently, semi-automatic designs derived from the Saiga
rifle family.

Case and diameter dimensions on this page come from C.I.P.'s published standard and are recorded
as verified. C.I.P.'s stated maximum pressure of 340 MPa is markedly higher than the 254.8 MPa
figure Wikipedia's infobox cites from a Russian ammunition-industry source; this page follows the
C.I.P. standards-body figure as verified and notes the disagreement here rather than picking a
value to call correct. A single bullet weight is recorded from the same Russian source, but no
test-barrel length is stated for its published velocity, so no velocity or energy figure is
recorded for this cartridge. No photograph of a rifle chambered for it appears on this site.
