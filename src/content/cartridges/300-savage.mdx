The .300 Savage was Savage Arms' 1920 attempt to fit close to .30-06 Springfield performance into a
case short enough for its Model 99 lever-action rifle's action, at a time when the .30-06 itself
needed a bolt-action's longer action length to feed reliably. Necked up from the earlier .250
Savage, it gave lever- and bolt-action hunters a genuinely flat-shooting medium-game round without
the longer receiver a full-length .30-06 case demanded, and it sold well into both rifle types for
decades.

Its main historical significance came after its own commercial peak: when the US Army and then
Winchester wanted a shorter cartridge to replace the .30-06 in a new generation of battle and sporting
rifles in the early 1950s, they used the .300 Savage's case capacity and overall length as the
starting point for what became 7.62×51mm NATO and its civilian twin, the .308 Winchester — a
cartridge that went on to eclipse the .300 Savage commercially within a few years of its 1952
introduction.

Every dimension in this entry comes from the C.I.P. datasheet for "300 Savage". Of the three factory
loads Wikipedia's infobox lists, only Hornady's 150-grain SST Superformance load could be
independently confirmed against a live manufacturer product page, stating a 24-inch test barrel; the
Remington Core-Lokt and Federal soft-point loads are cited only to a retailer (MidwayUSA) rather than
the manufacturers directly, so their bullet mass and velocity figures are recorded as estimated. Two
further loads in the infobox, a 150-grain FMJ and a 165-grain PSPCL, are cited to Accurate Powder's
reloading-data pages and are excluded entirely: those figures describe a handloaded charge weight
rather than a factory cartridge. No ballistic coefficient is published for any of the three loads
recorded here.
