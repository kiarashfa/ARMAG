name: ".218 Bee"
---

Winchester introduced the .218 Bee in 1937 by necking the older .32-20 Winchester case down to
take a .224-inch bullet, producing a small rimmed, bottlenecked round for single-shot and
lever-action rifles built to handle a light, fast varmint cartridge rather than the pistol-length
rounds those actions had traditionally fired. It arrived alongside Winchester's Model 65 lever
rifle and was later chambered in the company's bolt-action Model 43, giving hunters a cartridge
built specifically to shoot small game and varmints without the report or recoil of a full-size
rifle round.

The Bee's rimmed case suited the tubular-magazine and single-shot actions available when it was
introduced, at the cost of the smoother, box-magazine feeding that a rimless case like the .222
Remington would later offer purpose-built bolt rifles. That competition, arriving in the 1950s,
left the Bee as a niche cartridge rather than a mainstream one; it survives today mainly through
handloaders and a small range of factory ammunition rather than as a widely stocked commercial
round.
