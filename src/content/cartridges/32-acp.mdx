The .32 ACP is a semi-rimmed, straight-walled pistol cartridge John Browning designed for
Fabrique Nationale in 1899, first chambered in the FN Model 1900. It was among the earliest
commercially successful self-loading pistol cartridges: small enough for a compact blowback
pocket pistol, yet reliable enough for the police and military service pistols that adopted it
across Europe through both world wars, under its metric name of 7.65×17mm Browning.

At 17.2 mm case length and a 160 MPa C.I.P. pressure ceiling, it is a low-pressure round by the
standards of a modern service pistol, which is exactly what let it run in simple, unlocked
blowback actions with no rotating barrel or locking lugs. That combination — light recoil, a
slim single-stack magazine, and a mechanism cheap to manufacture — made it the standard chambering
for small European and American pocket autos for most of the twentieth century, a role now
mostly filled by 9×19mm and .380 ACP subcompacts.

This entry carries the full C.I.P. dimensional standard and one factory load, Federal's American
Eagle 71-grain full metal jacket rated at 274.3 m/s from a stated 4-inch test barrel. No free
source publishes a rifling twist rate tied specifically to a commercial pocket-pistol barrel, and
no ballistic coefficient for the round-nose FMJ bullet shown was found published anywhere free —
the cartridge computes a muzzle energy figure but no trajectory.
