name: ".30-06 Springfield"
---

The name is a date. Model of 1906, .30 calibre, replacing the .30-03 after two years because the
Army had watched Germany adopt a pointed bullet and wanted one too — the case was shortened by a tenth of
an inch and the round nose was thrown away. It then served as the United States' rifle and machine-gun
cartridge through two world wars and Korea.

Its case is the parent of an entire family. Shorten it and you get the .308 Winchester's ancestor; neck it
down and you get the .270 and the .25-06; leave it alone and it remains one of the most popular
large-game cartridges anywhere.

What the M1 Garand did to it is a specific mechanical constraint rather than a preference: the rifle's gas
system was designed around a particular pressure curve, and commercial hunting loads with slow powders can
bend its operating rod. That is a fact about the rifle, and it is recorded on the rifle's page.
