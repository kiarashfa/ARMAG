---
id: 577-450-martini-henry
name: ".577/450 Martini–Henry"
---

Adopted in 1871 for the single-shot Martini-Henry rifle, this cartridge lengthened and bottlenecked the
case of its predecessor, the .577 Snider, down to a .450 bullet. The name states two bore diameters rather than a caliber and charge: .577 for the parent case at its
base, .450 for the bullet the necked-down case carries.

Its early service life was troubled by its own case construction rather than its ballistics. The first
Marks were made from coiled brass sheet wrapped around a strengthening strip, a cheaper process than
drawing a case from a single blank, and that construction proved fragile and prone to sticking in a
fouled chamber. Reinforcement was increased through several Marks before the army abandoned the coiled
case altogether in 1885 for a solid drawn-brass one, the construction every modern rifle cartridge now
uses as a matter of course.

No dimensional standard governs an obsolete black-powder military round: the figures here are converted
from the reference work Cartridges of the World rather than a primary standards body, so they carry
`estimated` status throughout. No test-barrel length is published for the cartridge's era ballistics, so
only bullet mass is recorded for its load.
