---
id: 7-62x25mm-tokarev
name: "7.62×25mm Tokarev"
---

A copy of the 7.63×25mm Mauser, adopted by the Soviet Union in 1930 because the Mauser C96 was
already in Russian hands in quantity and the tooling question answered itself. What the Soviets changed
was the pressure: at 250 MPa it is among the highest-pressure pistol cartridges in common use, nearly
double the .45 ACP.

The bottleneck case is the reason it feeds so reliably in submachine guns and the reason it wears barrels.
A small, fast, hard bullet at close to 500 m/s penetrates far better than its energy suggests and expands
not at all, which is exactly what a 1930s military wanted and exactly why nobody designs pistol cartridges
this way now.

Chambering is a genuine hazard: the 7.63 Mauser will chamber in a Tokarev and the Tokarev round will
chamber in a C96, at pressures the older gun was never proofed for.
