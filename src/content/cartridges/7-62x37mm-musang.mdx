The 7.62×37mm Musang is a rimless, bottlenecked cartridge introduced in 2012, developed and manufactured by the Government Arsenal of the Philippines for use by the military in special operations and close quarter battle. It is unrelated to the Soviet 7.62×39mm.

It grew out of lessons learned from the Night Fighting Weapon System, and was intended for subsonic loads in close quarter battle and suppressed weapons at ranges to 300 metres. It is a wildcat inspired by the .300 Whisper concept and made from components the Government Arsenal already produced or procured: a 5.56×45mm NATO case necked up to take a .30 M2 flat-base bullet, a 7.62 mm boat-tail bullet or a Sierra MatchKing bullet, for an overall length equal to the 5.56×45mm NATO. It can be loaded for subsonic or supersonic velocities.

Like the .300 AAC Blackout, it can be loaded into STANAG magazines, with 25 rounds fitting a standard 30-round M16 magazine, and an AR-15 needs only a barrel change to use it. It is used in the Government Arsenal's 10-inch-barrelled Musang PDW, which is based on the M4/M16 platform.
