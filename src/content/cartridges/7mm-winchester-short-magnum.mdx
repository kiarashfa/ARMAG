The 7mm Winchester Short Magnum (7mm WSM) is a rebated-rim bottlenecked cartridge developed by Winchester in partnership with Browning, and introduced to the shooting public in 2001 according to its article, which dates the design to that year. It is a member of the Winchester Short Magnum family and is the .300 WSM case necked down to take a .284-inch bullet.

The article says the distance from the case head to the datum line on the shoulder is longer for the 7mm WSM than for the .270 WSM, which prevents a .284-inch bullet from being chambered or fired in the .270 WSM's .277-inch bore, in the same way the .280 Remington was made not to chamber in .270 Winchester rifles. C.I.P. gives a 53.34 mm case, 72.64 mm overall length and a 440 MPa pressure ceiling, the same case and pressure as the .270 WSM.

The article says the 7mm WSM has not gained the popularity of the other WSM cartridges, and compares its performance to the 7mm Remington Magnum, to which it is roughly equal, and the .280 Remington, to which it is slightly superior in muzzle energy; it adds that higher ammunition prices than those rounds limit its popularity. It quotes Winchester's claimed velocities of 3,225 ft/s with a 140-grain bullet and 2,990 ft/s with a 160-grain bullet, with no barrel length.
