The 7.62×45mm is a rimless bottleneck intermediate cartridge Alois Farlík developed in Czechoslovakia
during the 1940s, adopted in 1952 for the Vz. 52 rifle, the Vz. 52 light machine gun and the ZB-530
machine gun. Its case shape was chosen to feed and extract reliably across bolt-action rifles, self-loading
rifles and machine guns alike under demanding field conditions, an intermediate-cartridge design brief
similar in spirit to the Soviet 7.62×39mm being developed around the same period.

Czechoslovakia's independent path did not last: within five years of adoption, the country fell into line
with the wider Warsaw Pact and rechambered its service arms for the standard Soviet 7.62×39mm, ending the
7.62×45mm's brief run as a national service cartridge by 1957. Its published ballistics sit close to the
7.62×39mm it was replaced by, and roughly comparable to the American .30-30 Winchester with an equivalent
bullet.

All dimensional and pressure figures on this page come from C.I.P.'s own published standard and are
recorded as verified. Its one published bullet weight and velocity come from Wikipedia's own uncited
infobox rather than a clickable primary source and are recorded as estimated.
