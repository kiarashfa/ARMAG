The .25 ACP is a semi-rimmed, straight-walled pistol cartridge John Browning designed in 1905
for FN's Model 1906 vest-pocket pistol, the smallest of his self-loading designs. At 6.38 mm
bullet diameter and a 15.55 mm case, it is among the smallest self-loading centrefire pistol
cartridges to see wide production, built specifically for tiny blowback pocket autos rather than
any duty or hunting role.

Its low 120 MPa C.I.P. pressure ceiling and light bullets keep muzzle energy modest even by the
standards of a small pocket pistol — a trade Browning accepted deliberately in exchange for a
cartridge and a gun both small enough to carry concealed with no holster at all. It remained the
standard vest-pocket-pistol chambering across Europe and the Americas for most of the twentieth
century, alongside the .32 ACP one size up.

This entry carries the full C.I.P. dimensional standard and one factory load, Federal's American
Eagle 50-grain full metal jacket rated at 231.6 m/s from a stated 2-inch test barrel. No
ballistic coefficient for the round-nose FMJ bullet was found published anywhere free, so the
cartridge computes a muzzle energy figure but no trajectory, and no photograph showing the
cartridge beside a modern vest-pocket pistol was found under a usable licence.
