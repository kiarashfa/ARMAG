name: ".40 S&W"
---

A cartridge designed by committee and by circumstance. The FBI had adopted the 10mm Auto after 1986,
found it more than most agents could shoot well, and began loading it down; Smith & Wesson and Winchester
observed that if nobody was using the full-power load, the case did not need to be that long. The .40 S&W
is a 10mm Auto shortened to fit a 9mm-sized pistol frame.

For two decades it was the default American police cartridge. It has since lost most of that ground back
to the 9×19mm, on the argument that modern expanding bullets have closed the gap while the smaller round
gives more rounds, less recoil and cheaper practice.

It runs at 225 MPa, half again the .45 ACP's pressure in a case a third smaller, which is why pistols
chambered for it wear out faster than the same design in 9mm.
