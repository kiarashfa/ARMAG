The Soviet answer to the 5.56×45mm, adopted in 1974 with the AK-74 and reached by the same
reasoning from a different starting point. Where the American round was designed around a varmint
cartridge, this one keeps the 7.62×39mm's case head and shoulder position and simply necks it down — so
the magazine well, the bolt face and the receiver of an AK did not have to change.

Its 7N6 bullet is the detail that made its reputation: a steel core with a deliberate air space in the
nose, which shifts the centre of mass rearward and makes the projectile yaw early on impact. Soviet
literature is open about the design intent; this site records the geometry and stays out of the wound
ballistics argument, which has no agreed methodology.

The two loads below sit at opposite ends of the same case: a rifle round at 880 m/s and a subsonic one at
303 m/s, each with its own stated barrel.
