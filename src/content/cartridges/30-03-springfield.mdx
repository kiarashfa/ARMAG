The .30-03 Springfield is a rimless, bottlenecked rifle cartridge the United States adopted in
1903 alongside the M1903 Springfield rifle, replacing the rimmed .30-40 Krag and its
single-locking-lug Krag–Jørgensen action with a rimless round better suited to a Mauser-style
staggered-column magazine. It was the US Army's standard service cartridge for barely three
years before being replaced in turn.

**This is the direct predecessor of the .30-06 Springfield, not an alternate name for it.** The
two share the same rimless case head and bullet diameter, but the .30-03 was loaded with a
220-grain round-nose bullet at a longer 64.52 mm case-to-bullet-tip overall length; when the
Army's ordnance board adopted the pointed spitzer bullets that other armies were already fielding,
it shortened the case's neck and throat rather than the case itself, and renamed the result
.30-06 for the year of the change, 1906. Existing M1903 rifles and .30-03 ammunition stocks were
both altered or withdrawn as part of that transition.

This entry carries dimensions from the cited Wikipedia infobox, recorded as estimated because no
free dimensional standard exists for a cartridge withdrawn from production over a century ago.
One ball load's bullet mass is recorded, also estimated; no source states a test barrel length
for any published velocity figure, so none is recorded and the cartridge computes no muzzle
energy or trajectory. No maximum pressure figure is published free for it either.
