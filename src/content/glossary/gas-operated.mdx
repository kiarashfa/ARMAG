---
id: gas-operated
term: "Gas operation"
aka:
  - "Gas system"
short: "An action driven by propellant gas tapped from the barrel through a port, acting either on a piston or directly on the bolt carrier."
seeAlso:
  - blowback
  - short-recoil
---

Most self-loading rifles work this way. A small hole in the barrel wall, some distance ahead of the
chamber, lets a fraction of the propellant gas out while the bullet is still travelling. That gas does work on
something, and the something eventually unlocks and cycles the breech.

The three arrangements differ in what the gas pushes. A long-stroke piston is fixed to the bolt carrier and
travels the whole distance with it — the Kalashnikov, the M1 Garand. A short-stroke piston travels a few
millimetres, strikes the carrier and stops — the M1 carbine, the SCAR, the SVD. Direct impingement dispenses
with the piston: gas goes down a tube into the carrier itself, which acts as its own cylinder, as in the M16.

Gas operation needs a barrel port and enough pressure to tap. A weapon firing low-pressure ammunition may not
cycle at all, which is why gas guns are fussier about their loads than blowback ones.
