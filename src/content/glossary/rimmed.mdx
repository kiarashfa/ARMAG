---
id: rimmed
term: "Rimmed case"
aka:
  - "Rim"
  - "Rim lock"
short: "A cartridge case with a flange at its base wider than the body, used to headspace the round and to grip it for extraction."
seeAlso:
  - headspace
  - stripper-clip
---

The oldest arrangement and the simplest to manufacture: the case has a lip at the back, the chamber
has a step, and the round can go no further forward. The lip also gives an extractor something unambiguous to
grip.

What it costs is stacking. Put rimmed cartridges in a box magazine and the rim of a lower round can sit in
front of the rim of the one above, so the upper round cannot slide forward. That is rim lock, and it is why the
Lee-Enfield's ten rounds must be loaded with each rim ahead of the one below, why the Mosin-Nagant needs
Nagant's interrupter to hold the second round back, and why a belt-fed gun firing 7.62×54mmR has to pull each
round backwards out of the belt before chambering it.

Rimless cases headspace on a shoulder or a case mouth instead and stack without complaint, which is why almost
every cartridge designed for a box magazine since about 1890 is rimless.
