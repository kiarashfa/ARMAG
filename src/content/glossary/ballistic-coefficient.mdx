Not a property of the bullet alone. A ballistic coefficient is the ratio of the bullet's sectional
density to a form factor measured against a specific standard projectile, so the same bullet has different
coefficients under different standards.

Two standards matter here. G1 is a flat-based reference shape from the 1880s and is what most American
ammunition makers publish; it fits blunt flat-based bullets reasonably and long boat-tailed ones badly, which is
why a G1 coefficient often has to be published as several values for different velocity bands. G7 is a
boat-tailed reference shape and describes a modern long-range rifle bullet far better across its whole flight.

This site stores whichever the source published, labelled, and the trajectory solver uses the matching drag
table. Mixing them silently would produce a plausible and wrong answer.
