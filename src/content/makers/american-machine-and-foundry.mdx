American Machine and Foundry, known as AMF, Inc. after 1970, was one of the largest recreational equipment companies in the United States, with products as different as garden equipment, bowling pinsetters, research reactors and yachts. Rufus L. Patterson Jr. founded it in 1900 on the strength of the first automated cigarette-making machine, and it began by building cigarette, baking and stitching machines in Brooklyn.

Its place on this site is a single small run: the Deer gun, a CIA single-shot pistol, which a Forgotten Weapons write-up says AMF was contracted to build in a batch of 1,000. The company's vice-chairman in the late 1950s was Walter Bedell Smith, who had earlier held leadership posts at the Pentagon, the State Department and the Central Intelligence Agency; the Wikipedia article records this without tying it to the pistol.

Gaps: no dissolution date is given here, since the company was sold off in pieces rather than closed on a single date.
