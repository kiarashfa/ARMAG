Formed in 1970 around one product and one salesman. Gordon Ingram had joined Mitchell WerBell's
suppressor company SIONICS the year before; when a demonstration to the US Army suggested his compact
machine pistol might replace the M1911, a group of investors put up the money for a company to build it.
The Army did not buy it, the company stopped making firearms in 1973, and it filed for bankruptcy in 1975.
Its designs outlived it: the tooling and the name passed through several hands and the pattern is still
made.
